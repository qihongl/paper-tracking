LLM-Memory
When Context Changes: Understanding Update Failures in LLMs
Junyu Guo, Yuchen Fang, Shangding Gu, Costas Spanos, James Demmel, Javad Lavaei · arXiv (cs.AI), 2026-09-30 LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: The authors build Controlled In-Context Memory (CICM), a benchmark that tracks and re-uses updated variable values across conversations and agent logs — the same variable is restated with a new value while the old one stays in context. They then run component-level probes in open-source models (Qwen, Pythia) plus a one-layer transformer analysed mathematically.

Finding: Models exhibit "stale binding" — they answer with the superseded value even when probes can still decode the current one, so this is a selection failure, not a storage failure. The mechanism is attention drift: when attention scores are near-tied, several old values collectively out-mass the single current value. Redirecting attention to the current value, with no further training, corrects most old-value errors while preserving nearly all initially correct answers. This is the cleanest mechanistic statement yet of the failure the lingering-memory line of work has been chasing from the behavioural side: the information is in the KV store, but recency-weighted read-out loses to accumulated older evidence.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; the intervention is applied post hoc rather than learned, so it demonstrates sufficiency of attention redirection but not that attention drift is the only cause
Causal event structure shapes convergent reactivation signatures in human cortex and language model
Zihan Zhang, Yuting Chen, Qiuyi Liu, Xinyi Zhao, Xiao Ding, Xiaoyu Fei, … Bing Qin, Ting Liu (HIT / Shanghai Gener Arfysica) · bioRxiv, posted 2026-10-02 LLM-Memory Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: Direct human/LLM comparison. They combine topological analysis of representational manifolds with neuroscientific reactivation analysis, asking how event-level causal relations are expressed in cortical activity during narrative processing versus in LLM hidden states over the same material.

Finding: Causal structure modulates reactivation in both systems, but along different coordinates: the brain organises it temporally (angular gyrus, precuneus, prefrontal cortex) while LLMs organise it positionally. Despite that difference the resulting reactivation patterns share intrinsic manifold structure. The convergent-geometry / divergent-coordinate pattern is exactly the kind of result worth pressing on: if the manifold is shared but the indexing dimension differs, then position-versus-time indexing is the axis on which model and human episodic memory actually part ways — which is a testable handle on the forward-asymmetry work.

⚠ Preprint; "similar intrinsic manifold structure" is a topological claim that needs a quantitative comparison statistic before it can bear much weight
The Emergent Symbolic Structure of Artificial Neural Networks
R. Thomas McCoy, Paul Soulos, Tal Linzen, Paul Smolensky · arXiv (cs.CL / cs.AI), 2026-08-30 LLM-Memory Cross-cutting [backfill]

Recovered from the open-items ledger — missed by earlier runs; the compositional-structure vocabulary was added after the harvest window had passed this preprint.

Approach: The authors ask whether continuous vector representations secretly realise symbolic structure. They replace the network's entire representation-generating process with a closed-form equation instantiating a symbolic structure, then measure how much behaviour changes — in small networks trained to manipulate lists and in LLMs across arithmetic, logic, code and language. They also use the symbolic approximation to make targeted interventions on internal representations.

Finding: The symbolic approximation tracks the network closely enough that behaviour is largely unchanged, and interventions on the identified symbolic structures modify LLM behaviour in predictable ways — so behaviour is genuinely reliant on those structures, not merely correlated with them. This is the strongest available bridge between the symbolic and vector camps, and it matters for how one reads "memory" in a transformer: if the effective representation is a closed-form symbolic object, retrieval is better described as instantiation than as similarity search.

Schema-Episodic
A cortical–hippocampal communication undergoes rebalancing after new learning
Arron F. Hall & Dong V. Wang (Drexel) · eLife, 2026-10-01 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Simultaneous anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and dorsal hippocampal CA1 recordings in rats across pre- and post-training sleep, with a generalised linear model decoder to measure directed ACC→CA1 communication during sharp-wave ripples. They separate CA1 into superficial (CA1sup) and deep (CA1deep) pyramidal sublayers and add optogenetic ACC stimulation as a causal test.

Finding: ACC→CA1 communication pre-exists but is weakened during post-training sleep, implying that ACC activity reallocates which CA1 neurons contribute to consolidation. The reorganisation is sublayer-selective: it appears only for task-inactive CA1sup neurons, while ACC–CA1deep communication stays stable. Optogenetic ACC drive preferentially suppresses CA1sup while activating a distinct interneuron subset. The claim is clean and the design earns it — a cortical input re-balancing hippocampal population contribution after learning, rather than simply driving more replay.

Selective Hippocampal Reactivation Driven by Neocortical Feedback Supports Memory Transfer in Predictive-Coding Networks
Paul Saighi (Université Paris Cité) · bioRxiv, posted 2026-10-01 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: Hippocampus and neocortex are modelled as two coupled predictive-coding associative memory networks, with asymmetric coupling motivated by reported CA1↔PFC anatomy. This sets up a closed loop: hippocampal activity trains the neocortical net, while neocortical feedback biases subsequent hippocampal activity. The model is tested against uniform rehearsal on transfer of correlated memory content.

Finding: Coupling produces a self-organising replay schedule — neocortical feedback steers hippocampal reactivation toward content the neocortex does not yet support — and this transfers correlated content faster than uniform rehearsal, with no explicit access to the stored corpus. The interesting part is that selectivity is emergent from prediction-error dynamics rather than hand-specified priority, which is a genuinely different account of replay prioritisation from the salience/reward tagging literature.

⚠ Purely computational; no neural or behavioural data, and the "faster transfer" comparison is against uniform rehearsal only — no competing prioritisation scheme is benchmarked
Theta sweeps accelerate cognitive map learning by broadcasting information to unvisited regions
Tianhao Chu, Jiaqi He, Zhenglong Ji, Fang Fang, Si Wu · bioRxiv, posted 2026-09-29 Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: An entorhinal–hippocampal network of direction, grid and place-cell modules, in which theta modulation plus firing-rate adaptation generate theta sweeps and Hebbian plasticity lets place cells bind environmental observations. The authors run simulations and ablations against a recent result that decoded grid-cell sweeps alternate left/right around the animal's heading at ~10 Hz.

Finding: Dorso-ventral grid organisation yields multi-scale, independent sweeps that broadcast the current observation into a broad fan-shaped unvisited region, letting unvisited locations acquire place–environment associations without being visited. Declining theta power with familiarity acts as an annealing schedule — from broad learning to local refinement. Three testable predictions follow (theta modulation accelerates early learning; broader spatial correlation favours longer sweeps; sweeps shorten with familiarity). This is a concrete mechanistic answer to how a cognitive map is learned from sparse coverage — the same sample-efficiency question that motivates schema-based models.

⚠ Preprint; model-only, with the predictions stated but not yet tested against data
Structured experience shapes strategy learning and neural dynamics in the medial entorhinal cortex
John C. Bowler, Dua B. Azhar, Cambria M. Jensen, Hyun-Woo Lee, James G. Heys (Utah) · Nature Neuroscience, 2026-09-03 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting [backfill]

Recovered from the open-items ledger — missed by earlier runs; the curriculum/strategy vocabulary was added after the harvest window had passed this paper.

Approach: Recurrent neural networks were trained on an odor-timing task previously used to study complex timing behaviour in mice, under different training curricula (structured versus unstructured early experience). Model predictions were then tested against mouse behaviour and medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) recordings, with dynamical systems analysis of how curricula shape network dynamics as task complexity grows.

Finding: Without structured early experience both RNNs and mice develop rigid, error-prone strategies; structured training instead produces MEC activity reflecting the task's temporal structure and supports abstraction and generalisation as complexity increases. The two-species-two-model design is the reason this one is worth reading: the curriculum effect transfers from the artificial network to the biological circuit, so "structure of prior experience" is doing real explanatory work rather than being a training trick.

Neuronal selectivity and geometric alignment in the human hippocampus support abstract generalization
Armin Hakkak Moghadam Torbati & N. Davoudi (ULB) · bioRxiv, posted 2026-08-28 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting [backfill]

Recovered from the open-items ledger — missed by earlier runs; the geometric-alignment vocabulary was added after the harvest window had passed this preprint.

Approach: Two halves. First, artificial neural populations were manipulated by composition (adding stimulus-selective, response-selective, or category-selective units) to test whether merely increasing task-related information improves cross-context generalization. Second, human hippocampal single-neuron recordings were analysed for the same principle, plus mediation analysis linking neuronal class to geometry to generalization.

Finding: Adding category-selective neurons improves cross-context generalization while adding stimulus/response-selective neurons only increases encoding strength — so the type of information, not the amount, drives abstraction. Human hippocampal neurons show the same dissociation: category-like and identity-like neurons raise stimulus encoding comparably, but only category-like neurons raise cross-context generalization. Critically, the mediating geometric property is category-axis alignment across contexts, not the strength of category separation. That distinction is the part worth exporting — it says alignment, not margin, is what buys transfer.

⚠ Preprint; the artificial-population manipulations are the causal half, so the human mediation result remains correlational
KV-Networks
CyFA: Linear Sequence Modeling with Relative-Time-Partitioned Memory
Yixiao Chen, Shuojin Yang, Shi-Min Hu (Tsinghua) · arXiv (cs.LG), 2026-09-28 KV-Networks LLM-Memory

Approach: Linear RNNs keep a fixed-size recurrent state that must hold every past key–value association. CyFA adds a learned clock that, at each step, applies a cyclic transport jointly to the key and value states before the current pair enters the age-zero slot — partitioning stored associations across relative-time slots. The authors derive an exact change to absolute-clock coordinates that rewrites CyFA as two scalar-decay linear-attention recurrences, enabling chunk-wise training, and pretrain at 400M–1.4B with matched state sizes.

Finding: Recall-intensive performance improves while language modelling stays competitive: at 400M, CyFA beats KDA on FDA 42.60 vs 26.07 while using 46.7% / 48.3% of KDA's forward/backward core-operator time. The mechanistic point — that the fix for lost early associations is to give the state a relative-time coordinate rather than just a better forgetting rule — is a direct comment on how a KV store should be organised, which is why it is included despite the efficiency framing.

⚠ Gains are reported on recall-intensive synthetic benchmarks (FDA) plus LM loss; no mechanistic interpretability showing which slot holds what
Temporal Interference Modulates Medial Temporal and Frontoparietal Activity During Mnemonic Discrimination: A High-Resolution Whole-Brain fMRI Investigation
Min Sung Seo, Rebecca L. Wagner, Jordan D. Chamberlain, Kathryn L. Canada, Nancy A. Dennis (Penn State / UMass) · Hippocampus, 2026-10-01 KV-Networks Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: High-resolution whole-brain fMRI with a continuous recognition task in which the lag between an item's first presentation and its later target or similar-lure reappearance was manipulated at 10, 60 or 140 intervening trials. Behavioural mnemonic discrimination and target recognition were measured alongside neural lag effects.

Finding: Increasing lag impaired both mnemonic discrimination and target recognition. Neurally, however, lag effects were absent in hippocampal subfields and instead appeared in extrahippocampal MTL (parahippocampal, perirhinal) and distributed frontoparietal control regions — with partially dissociable patterns for successful discrimination versus target recognition. This is the most directly relevant human result of the run for the temporal-position work: it says the behavioural lag cost is real but that its neural substrate is not where the pattern-separation canon puts it. If lag/position effects live outside the hippocampus proper, a KV-style account in which position is carried by a distributed readout gains ground over a purely hippocampal one.

Encoding-Retrieval
Real-time fMRI feedback guides self-generated cognition toward upregulation of hippocampal activity
Sterre C. N. van de Langenberg, Guy Gurevitch, Taly Markovits, … Lars Schwabe · Current Biology, 2026-10-01 Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: Two real-time fMRI neurofeedback studies (n = 141 total). Participants used self-generated mental states to upregulate hippocampal activity within a single session. The key control is a yoked sham group receiving the same instructions but feedback unrelated to hippocampal state fluctuations; entorhinal cortex serves as an untargeted-but-connected region control.

Finding: Contingent feedback — not strategy engagement per se — is what enables upregulation: in the sham group, use of hippocampus-relevant strategies did not predict upregulation capacity. Contingent training enhanced capacity across all hippocampal subfields, with no effect in entorhinal cortex. Successful upregulation co-activated medial temporal and default-mode regions, and, notably, predicted multivariate pattern shifts at event boundaries during subsequent movie viewing. That last link is the actionable one: a trained hippocampal control signal appears to reshape boundary-related representations during naturalistic encoding.

40 Hz Transcutaneous Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation Enhances Verbal Free Recall in Healthy Adults by Modulating Frontal Theta Oscillations
Yinpei Luo, Shu Yin, Liping Chen, … Huizhong Wen · NeuroImage, 2026-10-02 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Randomised, single-blind, sham-controlled, three-arm parallel-group design in 44 healthy young adults (sham / 20 Hz taVNS / 40 Hz taVNS). Free recall was assessed before and after stimulation, with resting-state EEG recorded at baseline and at multiple post-stimulation time points to test frequency specificity and persistence.

Finding: Only the 40 Hz arm improved free recall relative to sham and to 20 Hz, accompanied by increased relative theta power, most prominently over frontal sites. The frequency-specificity (40 Hz beats 20 Hz) plus a simultaneous electrophysiological correlate makes this a cleaner neuromodulation result than most taVNS reports — though the mediation from theta to recall is asserted rather than formally tested.

⚠ n=44 across three arms (~15/arm); single-blind; theta increase and recall gain are co-occurring, not shown to be causally linked
Cross-cutting
Modeling decision dynamics disentangles working memory, cognitive control and reinforcement learning and reveals clinical differences
Krishn Bera, Alexander Fengler, Megan A. Boudewyn, … Michael J. Frank (Brown) · PLOS Computational Biology, 2026-09-30 Cross-cutting KV-Networks

Approach: Using the RLWM paradigm, which manipulates working-memory load during learning, the authors jointly model choices and response-time distributions under hierarchical Bayesian estimation, then compare parameter recovery and out-of-sample prediction against choice-only fits. Clinical differences are examined as an application.

Finding: Adding decision dynamics improves parameter recovery and yields accurate out-of-sample choice prediction in a held-out post-learning test phase, whereas choice-only fits produce inflated RL learning rates — a concrete identifiability failure that would otherwise be mistaken for a faster-learning subject. The methodological lesson generalises well beyond this paradigm: WM and RL contributions are not separable from choices alone, and ignoring RT biases the RL parameters systematically.

Top-down semantic predictions align phonetic neuronal dynamics in human superior temporal gyrus
Pavo Orepić, Wilson Truccolo, Eric Halgren, … Timothée Proix · Nature Communications, 2026-10-03 Cross-cutting Peripheral

Approach: Intracortical recordings from 624 neurons across three human participants with microelectrode arrays in anterior superior temporal gyrus, during an auditory semantic categorisation task and natural speech perception. They test a bottom-up cumulative account against a top-down semantic account of phonetic–semantic temporal alignment, and cross-check against adjacent ECoG.

Finding: Lexico-semantic and phonetic features occupy distinct low-dimensional manifolds (neural subspaces) with functionally separated dynamics, and phonetic activity aligns to word-level semantic representations — the signature of top-down prediction rather than feedforward accumulation. Effects hold at the spiking level across all three participants but are invisible in adjacent ECoG. The spiking-versus-ECoG dissociation is a useful caution for anyone reading population-level recordings as proxies for single-unit computations.

⚠ n=3 participants; effects absent in ECoG, which limits cross-lab replication options
CogGym: Towards Large-Scale Comparative Evaluation of Human and Machine Cognition
Lance Ying, Jinzhou Wu, Yingshan Susan Wang, … Kevin Smith, Joshua B. Tenenbaum · arXiv (cs.AI), 2026-09-18 Cross-cutting [backfill]

Recovered from the open-items ledger — missed by earlier runs; the comparative-evaluation vocabulary was added after the harvest window had passed this preprint.

Approach: A semi-automated, human-in-the-loop pipeline standardises experimental paradigms into a task-agnostic Experiment Markup Language, giving matched human and model trials. The initial release curates 258 cognitive experiments from 100 papers focused on commonsense reasoning and evaluates 50 LLMs against human responses.

Finding: Larger and more recent models reproduce human judgments better, but far more slowly than their gains on math and coding benchmarks. The headline number is the gap: best model–human fit is R² = 0.59 (text), 0.58 (image), 0.43 (video) against human split-half reliability of 0.93 / 0.95 / 0.92. Reporting a human ceiling alongside model fit is the right move, and the ceiling framing is directly usable as a benchmark convention for any model–human memory comparison.

More accurate behavioral predictions with hybrid Bayesian-connectionist models
Brenden M. Lake, Akshay K. Jagadish, Guangyuan Jiang · arXiv (cs.LG / q-bio.NC), 2026-08-23 Cross-cutting [backfill]

Recovered from the open-items ledger — missed by earlier runs; the Bayesian-connectionist vocabulary was added after the harvest window had passed this preprint.

Approach: "Bayesian distillation with Behavioral Tuning" (BBT) is a two-stage recipe: train a neural network to mimic a Bayesian model on synthetic data, then fine-tune it on human behaviour. It is evaluated across four case studies in human concept learning against both parent traditions.

Finding: BBT beats both Bayesian and neural-network baselines at predicting human behaviour while remaining interpretable — the resulting models both reproduce Bayesian priors and capture heuristics and biases that violate simple modelling assumptions. The recipe is a practical answer to the usual forced choice between a model with the right inductive biases and one with enough capacity to fit real data.

⚠ Preprint; four case studies all in concept learning, so generality across memory paradigms is untested
Peripheral
Different functional connectivity gradients reflect aging and Alzheimer's disease
Jonathan Rittmo, … Jacob W. Vogel · Nature Neuroscience, 2026-10-01 Peripheral

Approach: Functional connectivity (FC) similarity changes in two independent cohorts (BioFINDER-2, N = 973; ADNI, N = 129), analysed in the framework of whole-cortex hierarchical communication axes rather than preset regions or networks.

Finding: AD-related FC change aligns with the sensory–association axis, while age-related FC change aligns with the orthogonal representation–executive axis — and the changes are mirror-image reconfigurations at the two ends of each axis, not isolated increases or decreases. Sensory–association alignment is detectable at early pathology stages, even in cognitively unimpaired individuals. Large-N and replicated across cohorts and methods, which makes it a credible organising frame for FC findings even though it is several steps from mechanism.
