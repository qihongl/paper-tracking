LLM-Memory
Naju: A Native Discrete State-Space Model with Independent Retention and Writing for Long-Sequence Memory
Hyuk Lim, Seunghyun Yoon (ETRI) · arXiv cs.AI, submitted 23 Jul 2026 LLM-Memory

Approach: Argue that continuous-time SSMs such as Mamba waste parameters discretizing a continuous system for memory tracking, and parameterize the discrete recurrence directly. Naju ("Native Adaptive Junction Unit") factors the update into an explicit learned sigmoid forget gate (pole fn, 0<fn<1), an independent input-dependent write gain in, and input-dependent write/read maps — decoupling retention from writing. They analytically derive the structural constraint binding coupled single-gate designs.

Finding: Any non-expansive single-gate recurrence ties effective retention r and write gain w through ‖r‖+w≤1, so near-complete retention forces weak writing; decoupling fn from in removes this. Empirically, Naju is the only evaluated model strong on both retention and overwriting at 4× the training length, and it beats Mamba baselines on WikiText-103, Long Range Arena, and multi-query associative recall while keeping linear-time, linear-memory scaling. Mechanistically, this is a clean model of the retention–overwriting (interference) tradeoff central to Pillar 1's lingering-memory work.

AttriMem: Attribution-Guided Process Feedback for Agent Memory Learning
Qinfeng Li, Yuntai Bao, Xinyan Yu, Hongze Chen, Wenqi Zhang, Xuhong Zhang · arXiv cs.AI, submitted 23 Jul 2026 LLM-Memory

Approach: Frames memory construction as an RL policy deciding what to extract, store, update, compress, or discard as interactions accumulate. The bottleneck is fine-grained credit assignment — coarse outcome rewards cannot say which stored memory contents supported the answer. AttriMem augments the global outcome reward with local rewards derived from token-level attribution to the final answer, giving process-level feedback without a unique ground-truth target for intermediate memory decisions.

Finding: On long-horizon dialogue question answering, AttriMem outperforms retrieval-based, heuristic, and outcome-only RL baselines, generalizes across benchmarks and answer models, and stabilizes RL optimization. This speaks directly to Pillar 1: process-level attribution feedback for what to retain vs. overwrite is exactly the lingering-memory question of which past events an LLM should preserve under interference. (No numeric effect size reported in the abstract.)

Schema-Episodic
Replay of procedural memory is independent of the hippocampus
Emmett J. Thompson, Lars B. Rollik, … Marcus Stephenson-Jones · Nature Neuroscience, 23 Jul 2026 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Recorded neural activity during offline sleep consolidation of a procedural (motor-skill) task in mice, then compared replay features — sequence ordering, time compression, neuron-level prioritization — before versus after complete bilateral hippocampal lesions. Replay rate and content were quantified across the lesioned and intact states.

Finding: Striatal replay of the procedural memory occurred independently of the hippocampus: all replay features persisted under lesion (awake-vs-replay neuron positions, PERMANOVA group comparison P=0.922), and replay prioritized reward-linked over error-linked sequences in a way that predicted next-day skill improvement. The authors argue replay is a conserved, autonomous consolidation mechanism operating in parallel across distinct memory systems, not universally orchestrated by the hippocampus — a direct challenge to hippocampal-centrism relevant to Pillar 2's systems-consolidation framing. (Animal model; mice.)

Epigenetic and 3D genome reprogramming during the aging of the human hippocampus
Nathan R. Zemke, Bing Ren, Weiqing Xu (UCLA / UC Irvine / NY Genome Center) · Science, 23 Jul 2026 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Single-nucleus multi-omics — RNA expression, ATAC-seq, DNA methylation, and 3D genome (Hi-C) — profiled cell-type-resolved across 40 human hippocampi spanning the adult lifespan, to map age-related molecular reorganization of the brain's memory hub.

Finding: Microglia switch from an embryonically-derived to a peripheral-monocyte-like primed-inflammatory state between ages 50–75; astrocytes show age-related loss; and there is genome-wide erosion of 3D chromatin architecture with age. Together these define a molecular substrate for age-related hippocampal memory decline — squarely relevant to Pillar 2's interest in the hippocampal memory substrate across the lifespan. (Nature Neuroscience editor's note: linked via the PMC author-manuscript mirror because the Science DOI was not exposed at fetch time.)

⚠ Human postmortem, cross-sectional design (n=40 hippocampi across lifespan) — correlational, not causal; age effects are confounded with birth-cohort.
Causal network structure predicts memory organization and neural reinstatement across events
James W. Antony, Sara Abbas, Matthew S. Babb, Zachariah Reagh, Charan Ranganath · bioRxiv, posted 24 Jul 2026 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Represents naturalistic events as causal / shared-structure networks and tests whether that network structure predicts (a) how memories of the events become organized and (b) the pattern of neural reinstatement across events — extending the authors' "narratives as networks" program (cf. Antony/Ranganath on multi-event surprise and Reagh & Ranganath's cortico-hippocampal event-component reinstatement, Nat Commun 2023).

Finding: Causal network structure of events predicts memory organization and cross-event neural reinstatement, indicating that event-to-event relational structure — not just item-level features — drives hippocampal-cortical reinstatement. This is directly on-pillar for Pillar 2: schema/event structure guiding episodic reinstatement across distant events.

Encoding-Retrieval
Large-scale functional coupling and computational modelling reveal frontotemporal hotspots in distributed network connectivity during working memory recognition, encoding, and retrieval
Wuyang Zhang, Yuchen Xiao et al. (corr. Yuchen Xiao) · bioRxiv, posted 24 Jul 2026 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Intracranial local field potentials from 1,652 sites in 36 epilepsy patients performing a naturalistic card-matching task. Used phase-amplitude and phase-phase coupling, directed phase transfer entropy (information flow), and a time-delay-embedded hidden Markov model (HMM) to extract task-locked latent coupling states. Paradigm is naturalistic (game-like), not trial-based.

Finding: Widespread condition-dependent interactions, with frontal and temporal regions emerging as recurrent network hubs. Directed phase transfer entropy identified frequency-specific information flow — frontal regions act as a theta-band source and an alpha-band sink. The HMM recovered reproducible within-trial coupling dynamics and task-locked latent states in which frontotemporal hubs coordinate flexible transitions among distributed neural states during WM recognition, encoding, and retrieval. Relevant to Pillar 2 / Encoding-Retrieval (neural reinstatement, frontal-temporal WM control).

⚠ Clinical epilepsy population (n=36 patients) on a naturalistic task — caution generalizing to healthy working memory.
