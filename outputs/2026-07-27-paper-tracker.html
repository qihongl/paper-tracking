Schema-Episodic
Generative replay across hippocampal-neocortical circuits
Shearer, McDonald-Hill, Guillaumin, McGinnis, Lopes Dos Santos, Perestenko, Dupret, Barron · bioRxiv, posted 2026-07-24 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: Multi-unit electrophysiology and calcium imaging in freely moving mice across a multi-day inference task. The authors traced a putative dCA1 → primary visual cortex (V1) pathway via granular retrosplenial cortex (RSCg) and characterized neuronal activity along it during inference and in subsequent sleep, testing whether "generative replay" of unexperienced associations occurs outside the hippocampus.

Finding: During inference, V1 mnemonic activity mirrors dCA1 to represent learned associations. In sleep, V1 shows generative replay in which cells coding inferred relationships between cues never experienced together co-activate — and this V1 activity is predicted by dCA1 activity. The result positions the hippocampus as orchestrating a cortex-wide generative replay that could underwrite a hierarchical generative model extending beyond direct experience. Directly on-pillar for Pillar 2 (hippocampal–neocortical replay, generative models of memory).

Global structure of new relational knowledge networks is represented in retrosplenial complex, and node-distance in hippocampus
Feld, Fernandez Velasco, Gahnström, Bernard, Mian, Garvert, Dick, Gerchen, Spiers · bioRxiv, posted 2026-07-25 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: fMRI with a novel learning paradigm in which participants learned transition structure between fictitious, partially connected "alien planets" (individual pairwise connections only), then the next day viewed each stimulus during a cover task. Representational-similarity analysis tested how the learned graph was encoded in the brain.

Finding: Shortest-path distances within the learned graph were encoded in the posterior hippocampus and right retrosplenial complex, whereas global connectivity (closeness/betweenness centrality) was represented in the left retrosplenial complex. This extends the spatial-navigation system's principles to abstract relational knowledge — the hippocampus represents distance, RSC acts as a hub integrating newly acquired knowledge. Relevant to Pillar 2 (relational/schema memory, hippocampal–RSC computation) and to the researcher's naturalistic-RSA toolkit.

Cortical GABAergic dynamics around hippocampal sharp-wave ripples
Rezaei, Tohidi, Nazari, McNaughton, Mohajerani · bioRxiv v3, re-indexed 2026-07-26 (originally posted 2025-08-17) Schema-Episodic

Approach: Wide-field mesoscale imaging of extracellular GABA (iGABASnFR2) combined with simultaneous dorsal CA1 electrophysiology and sleep-state monitoring in mice, mapping GABA dynamics across 17 cortical regions during natural sleep and wakefulness; ripple-triggered activity mapping + SVD.

Finding: Sharp-wave ripples were associated with a reduction in GABA signaling followed by widespread cortical activation, both enhanced during NREM sleep. In NREM, GABA responses emerged earliest and strongest in retrosplenial/medial cortex before propagating laterally; in wakefulness responses were faster and lateral-sensory-first. The authors propose that SWRs recruit a cortex-wide GABA response whose regional expression and propagation direction are reconfigured by brain state — a dynamic inhibitory framework for hippocampal–neocortical communication. On-pillar for Pillar 2 (ripple/replay mechanisms). Note: re-posted preprint (v3), originally August 2025; newly surfaced in the 2026-07-26 collection.

No evidence for sleep-dependent memory generalization in a large online sample
Lu, Ji, Tompary, Schechtman · bioRxiv v2, re-indexed 2026-07-26 (originally posted 2026-03-03) Schema-Episodic

Approach: N=137 participants (Prolific) completed an image-location memory task across two sessions 12h apart, randomized to a Wake group (learn in morning) or Sleep group (learn in evening). 160 images from 8 semantic categories were positioned to probe category-based memory generalization/distortion; retention tested after the delay.

Finding: After the delay, retrieval was less accurate and more generalized, but these effects were largely independent of group assignment, with some evidence for enhanced generalization after wakefulness over sleep; generalization was also driven by time of day (more in the evening). The authors conclude a large online sample does not support a role for sleep in promoting memory generalization — a useful, well-powered counterweight to smaller-N sleep-generalization claims. Note: re-posted preprint (v2), originally March 2026; newly surfaced 2026-07-26. Single-task online design with no neural measure.

⚠ Preprint; behavioral only (no electrophysiology/imaging), so the null on sleep-dependent generalization cannot speak to underlying replay/consolidation mechanisms.

Cross-cutting
Developmental tuning of functional manifold dimensionality across the human brain
Busch, Turk-Browne · bioRxiv, posted 2026-07-25 Cross-cutting

Approach: Intrinsic dimensionality was estimated in five naturalistic fMRI datasets (N=781 unique participants, aged 3 months to 53 years) using T-PHATE, a nonlinear manifold-learning method robust to noisy, autocorrelated signals. The authors compared task-relevant vs task-irrelevant regional dimensionality across auditory, visual, and audiovisual stimuli.

Finding: In adults, task-relevant regions were higher-dimensional than task-irrelevant ones, but this modulation was absent in infants, emerged in early childhood, and strengthened logarithmically through adolescence. Crucially, it reflected a selective collapse of dimensionality in task-irrelevant regions (relative to a resting baseline) rather than expansion in relevant regions — a trajectory from global/nonselective (infants) to local/precise (adults). The study reframes functional specialization as developmental compression of representational geometry, a useful scaffold for the researcher's neural-geometry / representational-dimensionality interests.

Peripheral
Distinct forms of dopamine transmission control locomotion and learning
McGregor, Yee, Ekici, Power, Melani, Adler, Winborn, Kennedy, Tritsch, Ford · bioRxiv, posted 2026-07-25 Peripheral

Approach: Mouse striatal dopamine release was dissected into two spatially and functionally distinct modes and selectively manipulated. Eliminating the diffuse (bulk extracellular) mode revealed a spatially restricted mode that maintains phasic dopamine-receptor activation on striatal projection neurons; the two modes were then independently lesioned.

Finding: Selective loss of diffuse dopamine impaired striatal circuit excitability and reduced locomotion, whereas point-to-point transmission was sufficient to maintain spine density and support both motor and associative learning. The authors argue that the geometry of dopamine release determines its behavioral output — a principle that may generalize to how neurons multiplex distinct information streams. Adjacent to the researcher's reward-learning / RL pillar (G); included as a mechanistic-clean systems paper that may inform reward-modulated memory accounts.
