LLM-Memory
Temporal Context Reinstatement Drives Episodic-Like Order Memory in Long-Context Language Models
Mathis Pink, Vy A. Vo, Qinyuan Wu, Jianing Mu, Javier Turek, Uri Hasson, Kenneth A. Norman, Sebastian Michelmann, Alexander Huth, Mariya Toneva · ICML 2026 (arXiv 2607.22575) LLM-Memory Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: The authors probe whether LLMs solve episodic temporal-order memory like humans, using a Sequence Order Recall Task (SORT) built on a full-length novel (Agatha Christie's The Murder of Roger Ackroyd) with 97 human readers (990 segment-pair judgments) and Llama-3.1 8B/70B. They apply PCA to locate a "temporal direction" in hidden states, define a temporal reinstatement score (TRS) to pinpoint the responsible attention head, and run causal interventions (project-out / scale by 1.75×) on that head's retrieval output.

Finding: Both humans and models show the classic distance effect — accuracy rises with the temporal separation of two probe segments. The behavior is carried by a single attention head that builds a one-dimensional temporal code while reading and reinstates it at retrieval (L8H11 in 8B; L14H21 in 70B). Ablating the head's temporal axis drops SORT accuracy ~10–15% toward chance, while amplifying it improves accuracy by up to 9%. The effect survives scrambled-narrative controls (so it tracks position, not plot logic), and generalizes across Llama, Mistral-7B, and Qwen2.5-7B.

⚠ Ablation effect is moderate (10–15%); the "relies on" claim outruns a single-head manipulation, and a 1-D code may not generalize to richer event segmentation/causal reasoning.
E-mem: Multi-Agent Based Episodic Context Reconstruction for LLM Agent Memory
Kaixiang Wang, Yidan Lin, Zihan Wang, Bunyod Suvonov, Zhaojiacheng Zhou, Yuxiang Zheng, Jiaxi Cao, Zhiheng Dong, Chentao Wu, Jiong Lou, Jie Li · ICML 2026 (arXiv 2601.21714) LLM-Memory *Cross-cutting

Approach: Proposes a multi-agent LLM-agent memory framework inspired by biological engrams. Multiple assistant agents (Qwen3-4B SLMs) hold uncompressed raw text segments; a central master agent (GPT-4o-mini / Qwen2.5-14B) performs global planning. A multi-pathway router (global-alignment + semantic + symbolic triggers) activates assistants, which locally reason over raw text and return timestamped evidence tuples before aggregation. Evaluated on LoCoMo (multi-session dialogue), HotpotQA-streaming (≤200k tokens), and AMA.

Finding: E-mem reaches >54% F1 on LoCoMo, +7.75 points over the prior SOTA (GAM), while cutting token cost by >70% (43× normalized vs. brute-force long context); a 14B backbone even beats GPT-4o on long-context. The thesis: preserving raw episodic context via local reconstruction avoids the "destructive de-contextualization" of embedding/graph compression, keeping causal chains intact for System-2 reasoning.

⚠ Efficiency numbers rest on coordination-token accounting that is not fully reported (Pith critique); if assistant–master overhead isn't fully counted, the net savings may shrink.
KV-Networks
Reusable modular architecture enables flexible cognitive operations in the mouse brain and artificial recurrent networks
Yuma Osako, Greggory R. Heller, Mriganka Sur · Nature Neuroscience, 17 Aug 2026 KV-Networks Cross-cutting

Approach: Recorded neural activity across cortico–basal-ganglia circuits in mice during tasks requiring stimulus processing and memory maintenance, and compared the organization to artificial recurrent networks trained on analogous tasks. The question: are neurons specialized per content, or reused across distinct cognitive operations?

Finding: Mice flexibly reuse the same neurons for stimulus processing and memory maintenance independent of content — a "reusable modular architecture" rather than a content-specific map. Artificial RNNs trained on matched tasks reproduce the same principle, arguing that reusable coding is a computationally motivated organizational solution. This dovetails with Pillar 3's claim that memory systems reuse representational substrates (key–value / modular organization) for flexible cognition.

⚠ The cross-species comparison is analogical; the mapping from mouse circuits to artificial RNNs is loose and not causal.
Schema-Episodic
REM sleep prefrontal high-frequency oscillation chains mediate distinct cortical–hippocampal reactivation patterns compared to NREM sleep
Justin D. Shin, Michael Satchell, … Shantanu P. Jadhav · eLife Reviewed Preprint v2, 21 Aug 2026 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: In rats, the authors compared reactivation during REM versus NREM sleep using prefrontal high-frequency oscillation (HFO) chain recordings and assayed the resultant cortical–hippocampal reactivation patterns. The design isolates whether REM and NREM contribute differently to offline memory processing.

Finding: Prefrontal HFO chains during REM mediate reactivation patterns that are distinct from those in NREM sleep, pointing to dissociable offline consolidation mechanisms across sleep states. Directly relevant to the user's sleep-reactivation / systems-consolidation replication work; extends the replay literature beyond the usual hippocampal-ripple framing.

⚠ Preprint (not yet peer-reviewed); sample size not extractable from the listing — verify N at full text.
Encoding-Retrieval
Cross-region neuron co-firing mediated by ripple oscillations supports distributed working memory representations
Ilya A. Verzhbinsky, Jonathan Daume, … Eric Halgren · Nature Neuroscience, 12 Aug 2026 Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: Human intracranial recordings across bilateral corticolimbic sites during a working-memory task, examining how cross-region co-firing is modulated by WM demand and by ~90 Hz ripple-like oscillations.

Finding: Human neurons in bilateral corticolimbic sites fire together, modulated by working-memory load, and reinstate stimulus-selective firing when the two sites briefly oscillate together at ~90 Hz — evidence for a long-distance integration mechanism supporting distributed WM representations.

⚠ The ~90 Hz band is labeled "ripple" but sits below classic hippocampal ripple (150–250 Hz); terminology differs from standard hippocampal replay and should be distinguished.
Cross-cutting
Strong and localized recurrence controls the dimensionality of neural activity across brain areas
David Dahmen, Stefano Recanatesi, Eric Shea-Brown · Nature Neuroscience, 19 Aug 2026 Cross-cutting

Approach: An analytical and computational study of how brain connectivity — specifically the strength and spatial localization of recurrent connections — shapes the dimensionality of neural population activity across areas, without assuming a stationary target distribution.

Finding: Strong, localized recurrence controls how many dominant activity patterns (the dimensionality) the system expresses, allowing complexity to vary across time and behavior rather than being fixed. Relevant to population coding and representational geometry that underpins memory representations.
