LLM-Memory
In-Context Learning Amplifies a Latent Symbolic Circuit
Melissa Wessel · arXiv (cs.LG), 2026-09-28 LLM-Memory

Approach: Traces a hypothesised three-stage symbolic-reasoning circuit — abstraction, induction, retrieval — as in-context examples accumulate, across three model families. Uses per-head causal contribution profiling and cross-shot activation patching (donating activations from a many-shot run into a few-shot run), plus function-vector scaling and injection at 0-shot.

Finding: The circuit is detectable and functional well before the model reaches high accuracy. Per-head causal contribution grows up to 8× going from 1-shot to 10-shot. Cross-shot activation patching raises accuracy from 1% → 56% at 0-shot and 17% → 88% at 1-shot; function vectors injected at 0-shot rescue accuracy up to 86%, largely substituting for the induction stage but critically depending on an intact downstream retrieval stage. The rule-following infrastructure is present in the weights before any demonstration.

⚠ Single-author arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed; model families not named in the abstract, and no effect sizes with CIs
Memory Consolidation Flattens the Temporal Shape of User Facts
Sugam Panthi et al. (Rabab Abdelfattah) · arXiv (cs.CL), 2026-09-29 LLM-Memory

Approach: Constructs LAPSE, a benchmark of matched user statements differing only in temporal form (e.g. "I am driving a Peugeot" vs "The user drives a Peugeot"), isolating aspectual flattening — loss of the ongoing/completed cue during memory writing. Tests three writer models across 11 configurations plus three deployed pipelines (mem0, Graphiti, Letta), then measures downstream reader behaviour.

Finding: Flattening is systematically asymmetric: the progressive statement was flattened while its simple-present match was preserved in 244 of 381 pairs, and never the reverse — in all 11 configurations and all three pipelines. The lost cue is behaviourally load-bearing: changing only the stored verb shifted all three readers' estimates that the fact still holds, and when readers could ask the user first, two of three acted without asking more often on flattened notes. This is a clean, quantified retention/degradation mechanism in deployed memory systems, not a serving-efficiency claim.

⚠ Reader tests described as exploratory; 381 pairs is a small benchmark for a claimed universal asymmetry
Do LLMs Really Forget? Hidden-State Leakage in Model Unlearning and How to Fix it
Hadi Reisizadeh et al. (Mingyi Hong) · arXiv (cs.LG), 2026-09-29 LLM-Memory

Approach: Theoretical analysis of the gap between output-level suppression and representational erasure, followed by training generative probe decoders on hidden states at each transformer layer so leakage can be measured layer-wise rather than only at the output. Evaluated on TOFU, MUSE and WMDP against state-of-the-art unlearning methods.

Finding: Establishes a fundamental separation: the decoder can be made arbitrarily insensitive to sensitive directions, driving output-level leakage to zero, while the hidden representations retain the underlying information. Empirically, substantial layer-wise leakage persists under every method tested. The practical upshot is that behavioural forgetting tests are systematically over-optimistic — forgetting has to be certified at the representation level.

⚠ Preprint; "how to fix it" mitigation not described in the abstract, so the remedy is unassessed here
FactorEngram: Factorized N-gram Memory with Basis-Level Gating for Language Models
Bowen Yang et al. (Hua Wu) · arXiv (cs.CL), 2026-09-28 LLM-Memory

Approach: Extends lookup-based n-gram memory (Engram). Instead of treating each retrieved embedding as a monolithic unit in its own hashed slot gated by one scalar, FactorEngram retrieves sparsity-regularised coefficients over a dictionary of basis vectors shared across patterns, and scores the backbone hidden state against each basis vector to gate the corresponding coefficient before reconstruction. The authors also systematically vary where the memory branch is inserted.

Finding: Basis-level contextual gating lets polysemous patterns selectively read out only the context-relevant components of their memory, and replaces semantic-irrelevant hash-collision sharing with learned dictionary sharing. On 340M and 1B Transformer backbones it improves language modelling and downstream task performance; ablations identify insertion before the attention sublayer in middle layers as the effective configuration.

⚠ Abstract reports improvements qualitatively — no perplexity deltas or effect sizes quoted
Schema-Episodic
A neural network that maintains and retrieves memories based on context
Hayoung Song, JeongJun Park, Qihong Lu, Giacomo Vedovati, Monica D. Rosenberg, Zachariah M. Reagh, ShiNung Ching · arXiv (cs.AI / q-bio), 2026-09-29 Schema-Episodic KV-Networks Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: A recurrent neural network augmented with an episodic memory buffer, trained to predict upcoming scenes while watching naturalistic movies, with context inferred online by Bayesian inference. The critical manipulation is how inferred context acts on the model: low-rank modulation of the recurrent connectivity (the working-memory substrate) is compared against alternative couplings, and each variant is scored by how well its activity patterns match fMRI responses in human participants who watched the same movies. Episodic retrieval is implemented as a key–value system with self-attention that additionally encodes context.

Finding: Low-rank context modulation of recurrent connectivity gives the best match to human neural responses during the same naturalistic stimuli. Context also gates episodic retrieval: the model retrieves on context similarity as well as content similarity, and the context-modulated model learns to retrieve memories like humans substantially faster than a model without context modulation. This is a rare case where a single mechanism — context as a low-rank gain on recurrence plus a context-tagged KV store — is pinned down by both neural and behavioural alignment.

⭐ Probable own-lab paper — author list includes Qihong Lu, with M. D. Rosenberg and Z. M. Reagh. Verify; if it is yours, this is the headline item of the run: it sits on all three pillars (context-modulated recurrence, KV retrieval, naturalistic movie fMRI).
⚠ Preprint — not peer reviewed; abstract gives no quantitative neural-fit or retrieval-accuracy effect sizes
A population code for semantics in human hippocampus
Melissa Franch et al. (Sameer A. Sheth) · Nature Neuroscience, 2026-09-30 Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: Single-neuron recordings from hundreds of hippocampal neurons in ten human patients while they listened to narrative speech. Spike counts were fit with Poisson ridge regression from word embeddings (contextual and non-contextual), word duration and their interactions; decoding and population-geometry analyses then asked how neural distance relates to semantic distance.

Finding: Contextual word meaning is encoded in the simultaneous activity of neurons whose individual selectivities span multiple unrelated semantic categories — a distributed, not a labelled-line, code. Neural population distance correlates with semantic distance, but only for contextual embedders (GPT-2, BERT), and the population supports a multidimensional semantic subspace aligning most closely with GPT-2 structure. Neural response variance increases with lexical polysemy, and for highly similar words the semantic–neural distance relation inverts, consistent with contrastive coding.

⚠ n = 10 patients; earlier preprint version circulated February 2025 — the peer-reviewed version of record is 2026-09-30
Retrospective Grid Cells in the Medial Entorhinal Cortex Encode Past Paths During Spatial Navigation
Ruojin Liu et al. (Chenglin Miao) · Advanced Science, 2026-09-27 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Large-scale two-photon calcium imaging in freely moving mice during spatial navigation, with standard grid-cell analyses applied to position estimates shifted backwards along the trajectory. Controls include darkness and assisted (non-self-generated) movement.

Finding: Identifies MEC grid cells whose gridness score peaks at backward-shifted positions of roughly 15 cm — they encode path history, not current position. The effect is not a calcium-kinetics artifact; retrospective coding shows reduced spatial precision in darkness and is disrupted during assisted movement, tying it to self-generated motion and idiothetic input. Complements the recent prospective-coding result and argues the MEC code is bidirectional in time.

Mental Scene and Memory Construction and the Hippocampus
Edmund T. Rolls · Hippocampus, 2026-09-29 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Neurocomputational theory paper (no new data). Develops a continuous-attractor account of scene representation in the medial parahippocampal cortex based on spatial view cells, fed by a ventromedial visual scene pathway via prostriate cortex, and specifies how self-motion/eye-position updates shift the represented location within a scene when the scene is not visible.

Finding: The framework shows how the same machinery supports episodic recall, prospective thinking and autobiographical memory: the attractor holds relative spatial relations among features/landmarks, and the hippocampal system binds items into that scene frame. Included as a genuine theoretical contribution rather than a commentary — it makes specific, testable architectural claims about where and how scene construction is implemented.

⚠ Theory only — no new empirical data and no quantitative model simulations reported in the abstract
KV-Networks
Space versus Context: Competition for limited neural resources determines engram cell allocation in the hippocampus
Kensuke Chiba, Jun-nosuke Teramae · arXiv (q-bio.NC), 2026-09-26 KV-Networks Schema-Episodic

Approach: An information-theoretic framework that quantifies the trade-off between spatial and contextual information carried by CA1 neurons, given that a subset of place cells gets recruited as engram cells for individual contexts. The allocation problem is solved as a constrained optimisation over a limited neural resource.

Finding: Limited resources make contexts compete for place-cell recruitment, and solving the optimisation yields the fraction of engram cells allocated to each context as a function of that context's occurrence probability. The theory predicts a non-monotonic relation between context probability and engram allocation — a non-obvious, falsifiable signature, and a normative account of how a fixed-size code is divided between "where" and "which situation".

⚠ Theory-only preprint; the predicted non-monotonicity is not yet tested against data in the abstract
Associative synaptic plasticity creates dynamic persistent activity
Albert J. Wakhloo, David G. Clark, L. F. Abbott · PNAS, 2026-09-29 KV-Networks

Approach: Recurrent network models in which synapses are not static: ongoing Hebbian plasticity continuously sculpts weights around fixed random baselines. Networks are driven with oscillatory inputs (random phases across neurons), stimulation is halted, and the resulting autonomous dynamics are characterised both in simulation and analytically via the spectrum of the effective connectivity matrix.

Finding: After input cessation neurons keep oscillating far longer than any intrinsic system timescale, including the plasticity forgetting timescale — by roughly an order of magnitude. The mechanism is an interaction between neuronal and synaptic dynamics that creates complex outlier eigenvalues of the connectivity matrix. This gives a form of working memory with no explicit storage or retrieval phase and no prior knowledge of the input; the authors invert the mechanism to synthesise persistent oscillations with prespecified dynamics, a dynamic analogue of a classical Hopfield network.

Intrinsic Associative Memory on Riemannian Manifolds: Curvature, Capacity, and Emergent Modes
Krishnakumar Balasubramanian, Zhaoyang Shi · arXiv (cs.LG), 2026-09-28 KV-Networks

Approach: Builds dense associative memories intrinsically on Riemannian manifolds by casting recall as Epanechnikov kernel-density mode seeking, and compares a geodesic energy against a volume-corrected energy. Derivations cover capacity scaling, one-step recall, and the mode structure induced by pattern overlap.

Finding: Curvature is not a detail — it decides what is remembered. Geodesic memory always retains an isolated pattern, whereas corrected memory obeys a sharp Ricci-curvature threshold: positive curvature can erase memories in high dimensions while negative curvature reinforces them. Capacity scales as qβ−1/2 for retaining every pattern and qβ−1 for a typical one. Overlap can create memories: designed N-pattern configurations realise all 2N−1 subset modes, whereas random data at the storage threshold yields only a Poisson number — a rigorous statement about spurious attractors in geometric memory.

⚠ Pure theory preprint; no empirical or simulation validation described in the abstract
Encoding-Retrieval
Patients with amnestic mild cognitive impairment are compromised in temporal order memory
Mariana Varandas et al. (Sara Coelho) · Journal of Clinical and Experimental Neuropsychology, 2026-09-28 Encoding-Retrieval KV-Networks

Approach: 26 aMCI patients and 36 healthy older controls completed the Temporal Order Memory for Everyday Events (TOMEE) task, designed to preserve the temporal organisation of everyday activities rather than arbitrary list order. Performance was decomposed into items evoked, items in the correct position, items in the correct sequence, plus derived ordinal-positioning and sequential-clustering indices, and related to standard episodic and executive measures.

Finding: A group × temporal-order-measure interaction shows aMCI patients are disproportionately impaired at recovering correct temporal order, over and above recalling fewer items overall; the sequential-clustering index is specifically reduced. Temporal order indices correlate positively with Logical Memory delayed recall and negatively with Trail Making Test B time. The dissociation argues temporal-order/position memory is a separable early vulnerability, not an epiphenomenon of item-memory loss.

⚠ Modest N (26/36) and the report does not quote effect sizes or Bayes factors for the interaction
A ventral hippocampus–prefrontal circuit mediates the pro-extinction effects of adult hippocampal neurogenesis on contextual fear
Barbara Planchez et al. (Catherine Belzung) · Biological Psychiatry, 2026-09-28 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Inducible Cre-dependent iBax male mice with tamoxifen pretreatment to enhance adult hippocampal neurogenesis before high-intensity contextual fear conditioning (1.5 mA, 2 × 2 s footshocks). Defensive responses to the conditioning context and a conditioning-associated object were assessed with spatial-memory controls, and retrograde labelling was combined with projection-specific optogenetic inhibition of ventral hippocampus→infralimbic cortex versus →basolateral amygdala.

Finding: Enhanced neurogenesis reduced the persistence of contextual fear (faster within-session freezing decline, better next-day extinction recall) while sparing other hippocampal-dependent memory, and increased recruitment of the vHPC→infralimbic pathway. Inhibiting that projection did not impair extinction per se but selectively abolished the neurogenesis-driven pro-extinction effect — and inhibiting the basolateral-amygdala projection did not reproduce it. A clean double dissociation on the circuit that carries the effect.

⚠ Male mice only; high-intensity conditioning limits generality to the clinical PTSD question the paper is motivated by
Cross-cutting
Task-structured modularity emerges in artificial networks and aligns with brain architecture
Yuhang Wu, Shikuang Deng, Kangrui Du, Marcelo G. Mattar, Yifan Wu, Dani S. Bassett, Huajin Tang, Gang Pan (Shi Gu) · Nature Machine Intelligence, 2026-09-28 Cross-cutting LLM-Memory

Approach: RNNs trained on cognitive tasks under three regimes — single-task, simultaneous multitask, and incremental multitask — with systematic variation of task load relative to network capacity. Modularity is quantified and compared against biological brain-network statistics (clustering coefficients, edge-length distributions) and against models based purely on spatial/wiring-cost constraints; robustness is probed by selective connection pruning.

Finding: Multitask training increases modularity over single-task training, especially once task load exceeds representational capacity; incremental multitask training yields the highest modularity and maintains superior performance under selective pruning. Critically, the task-induced networks match biological brain-network structure (clustering, edge-length distributions) better than spatial-constraint-only models. The claim is causal and directional: modularity is an adaptive response to sequentially introduced task demands, not just a wiring-cost byproduct.

Peripheral
Multiscale Organization of Amyloid-Associated Vulnerability Across the Hippocampal Memory System
Xueting Cui, Thony Y. Liang, Micheal Adeyosoye et al. (Malek Adjouadi) · NeuroImage, 2026-09-28 Peripheral

Approach: Hippocampal subfield volumetry with covariate-adjusted amyloid-associated vulnerability scores, computed in two independent cohorts (1Florida ADRC and ADNI), with 10,000-resample bootstrap rank-stability analysis, cross-sectional clinical-stage comparisons, exploratory diffusion MRI and within-subfield occupancy mapping.

Finding: CA1 and dentate gyrus hold the two highest covariate-adjusted amyloid-associated rankings in both cohorts (ADRC: 5.06% CA1, 4.38% DG) with bootstrap-stable Top-2 placement. Included here for the AD-biomarker angle rather than a memory-mechanism claim: it argues against whole-hippocampal averages as an imaging biomarker and localises amyloid vulnerability to the subfields most implicated in pattern separation/completion.

⚠ Cross-sectional and correlational — no longitudinal conversion data; amyloid status by PET/CSF rather than plasma
