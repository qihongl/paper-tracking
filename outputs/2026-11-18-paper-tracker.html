LLM-Memory
Deep Latents, Long Memory: Latent persistence explains temporal correlations in transformer activations
Cagnetta & Acevedo · arXiv cs.LG, 2026-11-13 (preprint) LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: Defines the autocorrelation function of a layer as the mean dot product between its activations at two context positions separated by a lag, then asks what shapes the tail of that curve. Tested first on synthetic sequences with controlled hierarchical latent structure, then on real text across several LLMs with sparse-autoencoder features standing in as latent variables, plus an ablation of the most persistent features.

Finding: Slowly decaying autocorrelation tails grow with depth in both synthetic and natural data. In the synthetic case the growth is attributable to abstract latents that govern long stretches of input, and the persistence time of a latent code predicts the qualitative shape of the autocorrelation and accounts for most of the empirical measures. In real text, feature persistence correlates strongly with autocorrelation magnitude and persistence time increases with depth; ablating the persistent features reduces the autocorrelation.

Why it matters: This supplies exactly the missing operational quantity for the current project — a lag-indexed decay curve that separates "carries a long context" from "carries an abstract code." Persistence time increasing with depth is the model-side analogue of a context drift whose timescale is set by the layer rather than by the input, and it makes "abstraction" a measurable slope rather than a metaphor.

Instant Weight-Space Consolidation for Long-Term Memory in LLMs
Suri & Manocha · arXiv cs.CL, 2026-11-16 (preprint) LLM-Memory Cross-cutting

Approach: CLAM pairs a recurrent hypernetwork that compiles a stream of session activations into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass — no gradient steps, no generation calls — with a complementary episodic buffer that supplies verbatim detail at inference. A gated latent state carries knowledge across sessions at fixed size, so the parametric store does not grow. Evaluated on LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S with three open models of 2–7B parameters.

Finding: CLAM leads all agentic memory baselines by 1–45 points and all gradient-based parametric methods by 10–45, while building its memory 3–151× faster in wall clock. The two channels are demonstrably complementary: the consolidated adapter adds 12–15 accuracy points on trials where the episodic buffer misses the gold evidence, and still adds 4–5 points with an oracle buffer.

Why it matters: A working implementation of complementary learning systems in a transformer — a fast episodic store plus a slow, gradient-free parametric consolidation path — with the dissociation measured rather than asserted. The "adds points even with an oracle buffer" result is the clean statement that consolidation is not just a compression of retrieval.

Schema-Episodic
Causal role of human hippocampus in relational structure learning and inference
Pan, D'Ambrogio, Kingston, … Mahmoodi & Rushworth · Nature Human Behaviour, 2026-11-16 Schema-Episodic 🗂 data available 💻 code available

Approach: Participants (N = 36) learned a relational structure and then had to infer untrained relations when the environment changed, with whole-brain fMRI and computational modelling. The fitted model was used to simulate the behavioural consequence of disrupting the hippocampal representation, and that prediction was tested with transcranial ultrasound stimulation of the hippocampus in a separate group (N = 20).

Finding: Behaviour showed structure-based inference rather than item-wise learning, and whole-brain imaging identified consistent representations of the relational structure in hippocampus. Stimulation transiently disrupted structure-based inference as predicted (P < 0.001, Cohen's d = −0.89, 95% CI −1.41 to −0.36).

Why it matters: The inferential step in schema-based memory gets a causal handle. The design's strength is the model-to-perturbation loop: the predicted effect size is fixed before stimulation, which is what separates this from the usual lesion-by-correlation argument.

Neotenic expansion of adult-born dentate granule cells reconfigures GABAergic inhibition to enhance social memory consolidation
Chung, Alipio, Ghosh, … Ahmed (Sahay) · Nature Neuroscience, 2026-11-18 Schema-Episodic 💻 code available

Approach: Genetically targeted, synchronous expansion of a single cohort of 4-week-old (immature) versus 8-week-old (mature) adult-born dentate granule cells in mice, combined with slice electrophysiology, in vivo LFP in dorsal CA1 and CA2, chemogenetic manipulation of interneuron populations, and a social-discrimination/interference paradigm.

Finding: Only the immature cohort recruited fast-spiking parvalbumin interneurons in CA3 and CA2, increased feed-forward inhibition, reduced social memory interference and improved consolidation. Ripples in dorsal CA1 and CA2 increased in baseline power, amplitude and duration, with stronger rate modulation by social experience. Interneuron-targeted chemogenetics were necessary and sufficient for consolidation with and without the expansion, respectively.

Why it matters: A concrete answer to what adult neurogenesis is for at the circuit level: added immature cells buy feed-forward inhibition, which lengthens and sharpens ripples. Interference reduction and ripple modulation are shown to be the same manipulation rather than two parallel stories.

Music illuminates sociocultural dimensions of autobiographical memory
Turnbull, Kubit, Jakubowski (Margulis) · PNAS, 2026-11-16 Schema-Episodic

Approach: 233 undergraduates freely described autobiographical memories evoked by short clips of familiar songs spanning three genres and two life periods; memory content was compared with NLP similarity measures. Two control groups reported memories evoked by keywords from the lyrics (n = 121) and by short narrative summaries of the lyrics (n = 129).

Finding: Memories cued by songs from the same category were strikingly similar across listeners, varying with genre and release date. The lyric-keyword and lyric-summary controls did not reproduce the pattern, so within-category lyrical content cannot account for it.

Why it matters: A clean demonstration that a schema can be a property of a cohort rather than of an individual — the shared prior is a life script indexed by cultural artefacts. For any naturalistic memory model, this is a between-subject covariance term that item-level encodings will not capture.

⚠ Memories are self-generated in free response with no verification of the events or their dates; the inferential step from content similarity to shared scripts is interpretive.
KV-Networks
Symmetry in Time: A Representation Theory of Linear Recurrent Memory
Shikder, Dey, Minac (Muller) · arXiv cs.LG / math.RT, 2026-11-12 (preprint) KV-Networks LLM-Memory 💻 code available

Approach: Formalizes the state update of a linear state-space model as a linear representation of the monoid of input sequences, then characterises exactly when an SSM can track a group at every sequence length. On that basis the authors introduce HyperSSM, which adds quaternion channels while retaining linear-time inference and parallel training.

Finding: Diagonal recurrences multiply each state channel by a number, and those numbers commute, so the state records which inputs occurred but not their order; such SSMs can track only commutative groups. Any SSM that tracks a group must decompose into independent real, complex and quaternion blocks. HyperSSM solves S5 with 32 generators — where previous SSMs are at chance — using at most five state numbers, and generalizes to sequences 25× longer than training.

Why it matters: A proof rather than an empirical complaint: serial order is not a matter of capacity or training but of whether the state transition can fail to commute. Any linear KV or gated-recurrence memory that wants to carry position has to be non-commutative, and the quaternion construction makes the required algebra explicit and interpretable (the learned weights recover gyroscope calibration and qubit-gate rotations).

A single inference process underlies the behavior and neural circuits of working memory
Gong, Belledonne (Yildirim) · arXiv q-bio.NC, 2026-11-17 (preprint) KV-Networks Cross-cutting 💻 code available

Approach: MemGP is specified simultaneously at three levels: computationally, working memory is variational inference over a continuous function on feature space, balancing memory strength against a Gaussian-process prior; algorithmically, writing, storing and reading are posterior approximation with a finite set of inducing points; at the circuit level the algorithm is mapped analytically — with no training — onto a recurrent network.

Finding: The model reproduces load-dependent precision loss, attentional benefits during maintenance, inter-item attraction and repulsion, and delay-dependent drift. The analytic map deduces centre–surround connectivity, a property long assumed rather than derived in attractor models, and yields a structured combination of persistent activity and synaptic weights for holding items.

Why it matters: Capacity becomes the number of inducing points that all items share, which dissolves the slots-versus-resources debate into a parameter count and explains why precision and capacity trade off. Read as a memory architecture, it is a fixed-size key set with a normative rule for which keys to keep — the same object a KV memory needs, derived from first principles rather than fitted.

Keep Images in Sight: Visual Position Re-Anchoring for Long-Context MLLMs
Xu, Zhao, Sun, … Liu · arXiv cs.CV, 2026-11-11 (preprint) KV-Networks LLM-Memory

Approach: Systematically varies context length, redistributes a fixed text passage around an image, and changes the image's position without adding any text — isolating positional relation as a factor. The intervention, Visual Position Re-Anchoring, shifts the cached visual-key rotary phases toward a short-context reference before the question is processed, operating directly on the existing KV cache with no extra forward passes.

Finding: The positional relation between an image and the question directly determines whether its details can be accessed; retaining the image in context is neither necessary nor sufficient for access. Static re-anchoring improved 97 of 100 model–task combinations across ten checkpoints and ten benchmarks, with greater attention to answer-bearing regions across six checkpoints.

Why it matters: A retention/access dissociation inside the KV cache, and the position code is the gate. For the lingering-memory work this is a direct warning: an unchanged KV entry can become unreadable purely because of where it sits, so any "forgetting" measured by retrieval accuracy confounds decay with positional drift.

Encoding-Retrieval
Intervening contexts reshape hippocampal-cortical retrieval states and later source attribution
Hermiller & Davachi · Journal of Neuroscience, 2026-11-17 Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: Two fMRI tasks (trial-based, controlled). Participants encoded objects against contextual backgrounds, then did an immediate retrieval test in which each object cue appeared against either the original background or a different one, and finally judged each object's original context on a neutral screen. Representational similarity analysis tracked encoding templates into the cue period and the final test.

Finding: Shifted cueing reduced source accuracy and biased errors toward the intervening context, with no detectable difference in immediate recognition or associative memory — the manipulation changed attribution without changing item memory. Cue-period patterns were organised by the currently visible context, and on shifted trials were more similar to encoding templates for the cued than for the original context. Greater differentiation in posterior hippocampus, parahippocampal cortex and dorsal ACC predicted better later source memory; precuneus engagement and dACC alignment predicted individual source choices.

Why it matters: Retrieval does not simply reinstate the encoded event; it rebinds the item to whatever context is present. That is a mechanism-level statement that the context tag is written at retrieval, which is precisely the assumption a temporal-context model makes when it lets the retrieved context drift.

Posterior alpha/beta oscillations reflect oculomotor dynamics underlying subsequent memory effects
Wu, Popov, Beilner, … Staudigl · Nature Communications, 2026-11-17 Encoding-Retrieval 💻 code available

Approach: Four experiments combining eye tracking with scalp EEG or intracranial EEG during free or constrained viewing, plus an experimental manipulation of eye movements and a saccade-parameter analysis to link oculomotor events to oscillation dynamics.

Finding: The degree of visual exploration, not subsequent memory performance, was the consistent modulator of posterior alpha/beta power. Manipulating eye movements dissociated the alpha/beta reduction from memory, and saccade parameters predicted the oscillation; the accumulation of saccades over time reproduced the canonical post-stimulus alpha/beta pattern.

Why it matters: A well-powered deflation of one of the most-used encoding markers. If alpha/beta desynchronisation is oculomotor, then "encoding state" estimates built on it inherit the eye-movement confound — which matters most in exactly the naturalistic paradigms where gaze is uncontrolled.

⚠ The four experiments are heterogeneous in modality and sample; the deflationary conclusion is stated more broadly than any single experiment establishes.
Direction of oscillatory networks in the human medial temporal lobe reverses across verbal working memory phases
Choi & Han · bioRxiv, 2026-11-13 (preprint, posted 13 Nov) Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Intracranial EEG together with single-unit spiking recorded from the human medial temporal lobe during a verbal Sternberg task, analysed for directional (lag- and phase-based) interactions along the posterior–anterior MTL axis and between hemispheres, separately for encoding, maintenance and retrieval.

Finding: Theta and gamma interactions ran predominantly posterior→anterior during encoding and reversed to anterior→posterior during retrieval, while theta tracking working-memory load was stronger in left than right MTL and left MTL theta coordinated spike timing more tightly. Interhemispheric influence was predominantly left→right, with the same phase-dependent reversal along the MTL axis.

Why it matters: Encoding and retrieval are separated by the direction of information flow, not only by which pattern is present. That is a concrete physiological implementation of a read/write mode switch, and it is invisible to the RSA-style analyses that dominate the retrieval literature.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; directionality is inferred from lag-based coupling, which is sensitive to volume conduction and reference choice.
The role of prefrontal sharp wave ripple-like events in working memory and behavioral performance
Vahedi, Constantinidis (Daliri) · Brain Structure and Function, 2026-11-16 Encoding-Retrieval KV-Networks

Approach: Local field potentials from monkey prefrontal cortex during a delayed match-to-sample task, comparing a passive pre-training condition with the active post-training task, and within the task comparing sessions with low versus high behavioural performance. Ripple-band events were detected and aligned to spiking and to the ongoing delta rhythm.

Finding: Ripple-triggered spiking rose post-training, peaking at the first stimulus, the delay and the second stimulus. Post-training ripple peak frequency was higher than in the passive condition during maintenance and retrieval, and higher-performing sessions showed lower ripple power during the maintenance period. Ripple-band amplitude was phase-coupled to delta.

Why it matters: Cortical ripples behave like a memory-demand signal rather than a replay carrier, and the sign of the power–performance relation is the opposite of what hippocampal work reports. Either prefrontal ripples index something else (interference, or failed suppression) or the "more ripple is better" generalisation does not travel beyond the hippocampus.

⚠ Between-session correlational design with no ripple-specific perturbation, so the direction of the power–performance relationship cannot be attributed causally.
Cross-cutting
A theory of generalized vector coding and subicular function
Wang & Bicanski · Current Biology, 2026-11-17 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic

Approach: A theoretical paper. Disco proposes that subicular activity signals discontinuities in the continuous flow of experience, coded in a vector base, and derives the resulting selectivity for the cell types that have so far been named only after their apparent stimulus: boundary vector cells, corner cells, axis-tuned cells.

Finding: Geometric discontinuities — walls, drops, objects, holes, corners, curved surfaces — produce the corresponding responses from a single rule, which also makes predictions for 3D environments, natural scenes and mixed-modality coding. Discontinuity is not the same as "boundary": non-spatial discontinuities in behavioural state yield axis coding (activity along parallel maze arms), and tracking population activity signals event boundaries as discontinuities in neural space.

Why it matters: It puts spatial coding and event segmentation under one operation. If an event boundary is a discontinuity in neural space, then the segmentation literature's boundaries and the subiculum's vector codes are the same computation viewed at two scales — a much stronger claim than the usual "boundaries are where the model resets".

Theoretical frameworks for credit assignment via behavioral timescale synaptic plasticity
Cone, Clopath (Ponte Costa) · Nature Communications, 2026-11-14 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic

Approach: Generalizes behavioural timescale synaptic plasticity into a framework (gBTSP) that can be applied to unsupervised and supervised objectives in both feedforward and recurrent networks, with a derivation of how plateau events reduce task error, then tests the rule on place-field formation, attractor memory, trajectory matching and delayed non-match-to-sample.

Finding: Unsupervised gBTSP learns place-field maps in the feedforward case and attractor memories in the recurrent case; supervised gBTSP solves trajectory matching and DNMS. Crucially, the speed of credit assignment through gBTSP is limited by network depth or CA3-like recurrence, which the authors read as evidence that additional mechanisms are needed to explain BTSP-driven few-shot learning in naturalistic settings.

Why it matters: A negative result stated as a theorem-shaped constraint. BTSP's seconds-long window is enough for a single place field but not for propagating an error through a deep network, which sharpens the question of what else must be doing the work when an animal learns a complex sequence in one trial.

Microglia mediate motor memory decay through activity-dependent phagocytosis of myelin sheaths
Hu, Luo, Zhou, … Gu & Lang Wang · Nature Neuroscience, 2026-11-17 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic 🗂 data available

Approach: A mouse motor-learning paradigm with longitudinal tracking of engram reactivation and of the myelin sheaths associated with the memory, combined with oligodendrocyte-specific CD47 knockout and overexpression, microglial depletion, and separate assays of myelin versus synapse phagocytosis.

Finding: Motor memory decay tracked the loss of memory-associated myelin sheaths. Reduced activity of motor memory engram cells lowered oligodendrocyte expression of the "don't eat me" signal CD47, facilitating microglial phagocytosis and driving myelin loss. CD47 knockout accelerated decay, overexpression slowed it, and depleting microglia prevented both the myelin decrease and the accelerated decay. Microglia phagocytosed myelin sheaths and synapses independently.

Why it matters: Forgetting gets an active mechanism that is gated by engram activity — silence licenses erasure. That inverts the usual framing in which decay is passive and only consolidation is active, and it suggests a structural substrate (myelin) whose maintenance is itself activity-dependent.
