LLM-Memory
On-Demand Attention: Language Models Know When to Recall
Haibo Feng, Ruiqi Liang, Hanyang Peng, Shiqi Yu · arXiv cs.CL, 17 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) LLM-Memory

Approach: The authors ask whether a decoder's own hidden state already encodes the expected benefit of a global attention read, before that read happens. They train only a lightweight recall head on top of frozen pretrained weights — leaving the full historical KV cache intact — to selectively invoke global attention when predicted benefit rises, and implement GPU-side conditional execution in vLLM. Evaluated on Qwen and Gemma families, including hybrid-attention backbones.

Finding: Decoding states do contain pre-read information predictive of recall benefit. Selective recall recovered most of the accuracy lost under pure local attention while substantially reducing global reads. The interesting claim for our purposes is not the speedup but the existence of a separable meta-memory signal: the model carries a graded estimate of when its retained context is worth consulting. That is a testable analogue of a retrieval-orientation / recall-gating signal that could be probed with the same permutation designs we use for lingering-memory attention.

Long-Lived Characters, Local Inference: Incremental Memory Maintenance for Game NPCs
Zimu Xu · arXiv cs.CL, 16 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: A single-author study of memory revision rather than memory compression. In a quantised Qwen hybrid recurrent-attention model, the runtime removes superseded attention KV entries, computes replacement records at the true sequence tail, and preserves the continuing recurrent state and untouched KV. Tested via multi-update dialogue replays, fixed-input placement ablations, and attention diagnostics.

Finding: True-tail updates preserved current-state and historical bindings across eight scripted maintenance rounds; a placement case recovered the full-refill quantity in three reconstructions, while slot-preserving alternatives repeated a double-subtraction error. Independent block composition weakened query-conditioned memory selection without a uniform chunk-initial attention collapse. The paper's framing — that an inference state is a maintained, history-dependent resource rather than a disposable encoding of the latest memory text — is the same claim our KV-as-memory-store pillar makes, and the "attention distributions proximity alone does not explain semantic differences" result is a useful negative control.

⚠ Single author, no peer review, no external benchmark; ablations are scripted rather than on a public task suite
Schema-Episodic
Hippocampal astrocytic sequences emerge during learning and memory recall
Ryan A. Senne, Rebecca L. Suthard, Rui Cao, Siria Coello, Amy H. Monasterio, Evan A. Ruesch, Michelle D. Buzharsky, Marc W. Howard, Steve Ramirez · Nature Neuroscience, 16 Sep 2026 Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: One-photon calcium imaging of dorsal CA1 astrocytes tracked across days in freely moving mice during contextual fear conditioning, with a non-parametric event-detection method applied to identify calcium-event sequences and a separate new context as a specificity control.

Finding: Foot shock evoked robust astrocytic calcium-event sequences with a time-compressed structure resembling pyramidal time-cell activity. Similar sequences re-emerged on later exposure to the conditioned context despite no shock, and were not detected in a distinct context; spontaneous astrocytic activity aligned with behavioural-state transitions including freezing. The mechanistic implication is that the temporal scaffolding of an associative memory is carried by a distributed, multi-cell-type ensemble rather than by pyramidal cells alone. Note: Marc Howard is a co-author; this sits directly on the temporal-context line of work that motivates the TCM program.

Cellular code for mnemonic pattern separation in the human hippocampus is revealed by false memories
Natalia Kurilenko, Clayton Mosher, Sophia Cheng, Yousef Salimpour, Jonathan Daume, Chrystal M. Reed, William S. Anderson, Taufik A. Valiante, Adam N. Mamelak, Ueli Rutishauser · bioRxiv, 20 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) Schema-Episodic

Approach: Single-neuron recordings from 3,506 neurons across 97 patients performing a recognition-memory task in which novel images similar to previously seen images produce false memories. Two cell classes were isolated: one differentiating falsely recognised novel from correctly recognised familiar items, and one signalling the subject's choice.

Finding: The first class behaves as the long-sought single-cell correlate of pattern separation in the human hippocampus; at the population level these cells predicted mnemonic ground truth in hippocampus while choice signals localised to pre-supplementary motor area, tracing a mnemonic-to-decision progression. Removing pattern-separation-signalling cells abolished the continuous memory-strength gradient present in hippocampus. This is the cleanest human cellular evidence yet that pattern separation is an operating computation, not a modelling convenience — and it is directly relevant to confusable-lure designs in our clinical single-trial work.

⚠ Large neuron count but the task is a single recognition paradigm; patients are epilepsy/intracranial cases
Juvenile rat's sleep enables schema memory across episodes before expression of memory for individual episodes
Julia Fechner, Jan Born, Marion Inostroza · bioRxiv, 16 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) Schema-Episodic

Approach: Postnatal day 25 pups (n = 24) were tested in an adapted object-place paradigm allowing abstraction of a spatial regularity across eight consecutive episodes; after encoding they either slept or stayed awake for 2 h, with schema memory tested 22 h later against pseudorandomised-configuration controls. A separate group (n = 12) was tested on standard single-episode object-place recognition with post-encoding sleep.

Finding: Schema memory was expressed only when spatial regularities were present and sleep followed encoding. Critically, the single-episode group showed no episodic object-place memory at a 4 h test, so schema abstraction emerged under conditions where robust single-episode memory was not yet expressible. This dissociates schema formation from mature hippocampal episodic coding and is a useful developmental constraint on the schema-binding account: the generalisation is not simply pooled episodic traces.

Goal location beta oscillations and sharp wave ripples are complementary markers of learning-related changes in hippocampal–neocortical networks
Sameera Shridhar, Zachary M. Leveroni, Nan Zhou, Ellie Quattrocchi, Audrey Kaye, Jaquelin Gutierrez, Amanda Rodríguez León, Leslie M. Kay, Jai Y. Yu · eNeuro, 16 Sep 2026 Schema-Episodic

Approach: Simultaneous local field potential recordings from dorsal hippocampus (CA1) and prefrontal cortex in male rats learning spatial navigation tasks, tracked across days of training as animals reached and stabilised goal locations.

Finding: PFC beta (~20 Hz) oscillations at goal entry increased in power with performance across days, whereas hippocampal SWR rate and size decreased over the same period. Goal-entry beta correlated with reward likelihood in PFC but not in CA1. The interpretive claim is that neocortical beta indexes the progressive consolidation of a learned task structure while hippocampal SWR indexes the still-active encoding regime — the two are inverse markers of the same hippocampal–neocortical reorganisation. A caveat for the schema literature: this is a correlation between two markers across training, not a manipulation of either.

Median raphe input to dorsal CA1 shapes VIP interneuron recruitment and novelty-guided spatial memory
Xiao Luo, Risna Radhakrishnan, Alexandre Guet-McCreight, Dimitry Topolnik, Aurelie Bergeron, Vandana Dulam, Louis David Coulombe, Megan Besner, Justine Fortin-Houde, Judy Davies, Felix Michaud, Benedicte Amilhon, Lisa Topolnik · bioRxiv, 18 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Combined anatomical mapping, optogenetics, transcriptomics, pharmacology, in vivo calcium imaging and a hippocampal circuit model to characterise median raphe (MnR) projections onto dorsal CA1 VIP-expressing interneurons during novel experience.

Finding: MnR axons innervate multiple VIP-IN subtypes and act through fast glutamatergic excitation plus serotonin-receptor-dependent modulation. Novelty preferentially recruited a speed-coupled VIP-IN ensemble, and inhibiting MnR input selectively reduced that response. Behaviourally, inhibition preserved exploratory engagement but disrupted the organisation of spatial sampling and impaired object-location memory; modelling linked the pathway to dendritic disinhibition and place-cell recruitment. This supplies a long-range neuromodulatory input that selectively gates novelty encoding — relevant to how encoding strength, rather than encoding presence, is set at the hippocampal input stage.

KV-Networks
Decoding Medial Entorhinal Cortical Dynamics Produces Planning-Like Alternations in Hippocampal theta Sequences
Masahiro Nakano, Caswell Barry, Claudia Clopath · Hippocampus, 18 Sep 2026 KV-Networks Schema-Episodic

Approach: A model of medial entorhinal cortex theta dynamics in a T-maze, decoded with a standard Bayesian decoder to ask whether hippocampal theta sequences that alternate between arms require a planning process at all. The model predictions were then tested against existing simultaneous MEC–hippocampal recordings (Vollan et al., 2025).

Finding: Bayesian decoding of MEC theta dynamics alone produces hippocampal theta sequences that alternate between maze arms. In the real recordings, MEC and hippocampus were tightly coupled with coordinated left–right alternation during an alternation task, and — importantly — this conjoint alternation occurred not only at choice points but also inbound and on forced-turn trials. The absence of choice-specificity is the load-bearing result: it challenges the reading of theta-sequence alternation as an intentional planning or prospective-decision signal. For the KV/temporal-context account this is a caution about over-attributing content to position-indexed sequences; the alternation may be inherited from upstream dynamics rather than generated as a trajectory plan.

⚠ Reanalysis of one existing dataset plus a model; the "ubiquitous" claim rests on that dataset's task design
Contiguity, Not Importance: Budgeted Repair of Stale KV Caches After Document Edits
Mingyang Mao, Wyatt Mackey, Xiaomin Lin · arXiv cs.AI, 16 Sep 2026 (preprint, not peer reviewed) KV-Networks LLM-Memory

Approach: The paper frames KV-cache invalidation after an edit as budgeted recomputation: under causal self-attention a local edit corrupts downstream KV states, and one must choose which positions to recompute. Training-free position-selection policies were compared on a factual RAG benchmark with matched direct and derived edits, across three model families.

Finding: At the primary budget a contiguous edit-local window recovered at least 0.94 of the post-edit answer margin and substantially outperformed attention-based, KV-deviation and structural selectors. The mechanistic result is the interesting one: position sets that look effective under clean-state transplantation fail under actual recomputation because scattered positions inherit surrounding staleness. The edit-local advantage depended on adjacency and largely disappeared once answer-bearing text moved downstream. Repair was 13–21× faster than full re-prefill. The structural claim — that contiguity of cached state, rather than semantic importance of individual entries, governs what a key–value store can recover — is a machine-side restatement of the contiguity effect, and it is the kind of concrete retention/retrieval claim that justifies including an otherwise applied paper.

⚠ Preprint; evaluated on a single factual-RAG benchmark; "contiguity" here means positional adjacency in the cache, not retrieved-pair contiguity in the memory literature
Do semantic and phonological categories lead to proactive interference in immediate and delayed serial recall tasks?
Sho Ishiguro, René-Pierre Sonier, Dominic Guitard, et al. · Memory & Cognition, 16 Sep 2026 KV-Networks

Approach: Five experiments (immediate and delayed serial recall) applying separate item and order scoring, with semantic versus phonological category manipulations to test whether inconsistent proactive-interference findings reflect an item/order confound.

Finding: Proactive interference was consistently observed in item memory for semantic categories (release from PI following category shifts) in both immediate and delayed recall, and in item memory for phonological categories only under delayed recall. No clear PI effect appeared in order memory in any experiment. The item/order dissociation is the contribution: interference is a property of item codes, not of the order/position code that the temporal-context account is built around. Worth holding against models that route both item and order information through a single context vector — the present data suggest the interference signature lives on the item side.

Encoding-Retrieval
Event segmentation and encoding rely on information accumulated over different temporal scales
Karen Sasmita, Khena M. Swallow · Memory & Cognition, 18 Sep 2026 Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: Participants segmented and then recalled video excerpts presented uninterrupted, interrupted by a white-noise display every minute, or interrupted and jumbled by playing the one-minute clips out of order — a direct manipulation of the ability to accumulate coherent information over time. Segmentation and recall were analysed separately, including fine-grained (sub-second/5 s) segmentation-rate dynamics.

Finding: Interrupting or jumbling did not significantly change overall segmentation rate, segmentation patterns, or the tendency to organise events hierarchically. A finer-grained analysis showed the segmentation rate briefly increased after a jumbled clip and stabilised after ~5 s. In contrast, jumbling every minute significantly reduced the number of events recalled and their organisation with respect to both time and semantics. The dissociation is the finding: content-based segmentation stabilises on a short timescale and is robust to narrative discontinuity, whereas memory encoding depends on longer-timescale integration. Naturalistic-paradigm relevant — it implies event-boundary measures are not a proxy for encoding success, which matters for anyone using boundary-locked responses as an encoding index.

Neural Reinstatement of Features in Audiovisual Working Memory Indicates Object-based Retrieval
Ceren Arslan, Daniel Schneider, Stephan Getzmann, et al. · Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience, 15 Sep 2026 (backfill — published inside the previous run's nominal window but not reported) Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: EEG audiovisual delayed-match-to-sample task. On each trial participants memorised an audiovisual item; at test they received a randomly chosen auditory, visual, or audiovisual probe and judged whether it matched. Multivariate pattern analysis was used to ask whether attentional selection acts on unisensory features or on bound cross-modal objects.

Finding: Cueing a single unisensory feature produced worse behavioural performance and elevated midfrontal theta power than cueing the whole object. MVPA showed task-irrelevant feature representations were nonetheless retrieved on both auditory and visual probe trials — i.e. selection of one feature spreads to the other modality. The proposal is that audiovisual working-memory contents are stored as bound objects, so partial cuing necessarily reinstates the rest. The midfrontal theta increase on partial cues is a useful physiological marker for the cost of partial retrieval.

Cross-cutting
The continued influence effect and situation models: Evidence for the model-updating account
Carolin V. Hey, Marie Luisa Schaper, Ute J. Bayen · Memory & Cognition, 16 Sep 2026 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic

Approach: N = 435 participants read a fictitious news report in which original information and its retraction were attributed to sources of high or low credibility, with credibility disclosed either before or after processing (so credibility could or could not influence situation-model construction). Participants' situation models were measured directly and entered into a structural equation model predicting the continued influence effect.

Finding: Measured situation models mediated the effects of source and retractor credibility on continued influence, supporting the model-updating account over accounts that treat continued influence as residual item memory. The methodological value for our purposes is the move of measuring the event model rather than inferring it: it makes "the schema was not updated" a directly testable mediator rather than a post-hoc explanation, which is exactly the inferential gap in most schema-binding claims.

Peripheral
Intracranial recordings in humans reveal differential contributions of medial and lateral orbitofrontal cortex to approach–avoidance decision-making
Clara Kwon Starkweather, Ethan H. Willbrand, Kristin Sellers, Patrick W. Hullett, Andrew D. Krystal, et al. · Nature Neuroscience, 15 Sep 2026 (backfill) Peripheral

Approach: Intracranial sEEG recordings from the orbitofrontal cortex of patients playing a gamified approach–avoidance task in which corridors contained varying numbers of treasure chests and bombs; participants decided within a fixed window whether to approach or avoid.

Finding: Two anatomically distinct pre-decision signals within OFC: medial compartment activity increased and lateral compartment activity decreased before approach. On difficult trials these signals rapidly alternated between discrete pro-approach and pro-avoidant states rather than settling, which the authors read as a real-time conflict architecture in OFC. Peripheral to the memory pillars, but the alternating-bistable-states motif is worth keeping in view given our interest in how a system settles between competing retrieved states — and the task is a continuous, dynamic choice paradigm rather than a trial-based one.

⚠ n = 6 sEEG patients (only 3 areas recorded in ≥5 participants); electrode placement dictated by clinical need
