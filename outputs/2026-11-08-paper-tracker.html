LLM-Memory
A Circuit for In-Context Learning
Andrew Gao & William Gao · arXiv cs.CL, 2026-11-03 LLM-MemoryKV-Networks

Approach: Mechanistic circuit analysis of few-shot sentence classification with arbitrary labels in three 3-4B transformers: activation patching, head ablations against random-head baselines, and steering with the models' own signals, plus a two-constant-per-head softmax readout model fit on one prompt distribution and tested on another.

Finding: ICL is a gated associative lookup. Middle-layer heads write the preceding demonstration's class as a key into label-token residual streams; upper-layer heads match the query's class to those keys and copy out the labels. An early-layer 'any label repeated' signal gates the readout (without it, readers default to the prompt start), and a separate head group suppresses the most recently seen label. The circuit is conserved across three models, reproduced from the named heads alone, emerges in a transformer trained from scratch, and persists from 0.6B to 8B and through instruction tuning.

⚠ arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed; the circuit is established on few-shot sentence classification with arbitrary labels, not on naturalistic long-context memory.
Where Should a KV-Cache Repair Budget Go? Distractor Structure and Contextual Compatibility in Non-Prefix Reuse
Yuxing Peng et al. (Weijia Jia) · arXiv cs.CL, 2026-11-04 LLM-MemoryKV-Networks

Approach: Controlled manipulation of the chunks surrounding the answer-bearing target under non-prefix KV-cache reuse across three 7B-8B models (Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B) and four datasets (LoCoMo dialogue memory, NQ-Open, TriviaQA, Gorilla); scoring only questions full prefill answers that require retrieved context, with a 5% recomputation budget.

Finding: Reuse loses 19-100% of context-dependent questions, and similarity-matched distractors make it significantly worse (9/12 settings). Recoverable damage is spread over distractors, so the budget belongs on the target: recomputing the target against the reused cache beats inserting its full-prefill KV in all seven settings even though the recomputed KV stays 0.23-0.30 relative distance away — what matters is encoding consistency, not approaching reference values. Question attention identifies the target in at most 18.8% of cases; moving the retriever's top chunk adjacent to the question and recomputing it beats reordering in 6/9 settings, but the effect does not transfer to dialogue memory.

⚠ arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed; the target-first repair recipe fails on dialogue memory, where whole sessions exceed the 5% budget.
Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout
Tushaar Gangavarapu et al. (Clemens J. S. Schaefer) · arXiv cs.LG, 2026-11-05 LLM-MemoryKV-Networks

Approach: Sensitivity analysis of gated delta-rule linear-attention states (166M-1.34B parameters) followed by an architecture change: fuse erase/write gates into a sensitivity-allocated low-rank projection, re-weight state rows with a matched-filter readout gate, and reinvest saved parameters in a wider FFN; a parallel update is derived because chunkwise algorithms become unstable at large chunk sizes.

Finding: State updates are far less sensitive to erasure than to writes — key energy concentrates on a sparse channel subset while value writes scale all channels — and slow-decaying rows accumulate much larger norms than fast rows, dominating unweighted readout and forcing stronger write-time erasure to suppress interference. Re-weighting the readout by those norms lowers perplexity 1.8-2.0% over GDN-2 and 2.5-3.6% over Transformer, raises commonsense accuracy up to 1.9 points, extrapolates 2,560x from 4K training to 10M-token streams with no perplexity drift, and holds near-100% passkey retrieval to 1M tokens.

⚠ arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed; extrapolation and recall gains are reported on synthetic needle/multi-hop probes, not on naturalistic retention.
Schema-Episodic
Persistent hippocampal traces of lifelong spatial memory
J. Wu et al. (M. Meister, C. Lois) · bioRxiv (Caltech), 2026-11-06 Schema-Episodic

Abstract-only screening hit recovered by the bioRxiv single-DOI API: the DOI stem (10-30) is eleven days earlier than the true posting date (11-06).

Approach: Mice spent two days in a binary-tree maze, then returned after repeated 90-day absences over as much as a year; chronic calcium imaging tracked the same CA1 neurons across visits while navigation efficiency and direct runs to the reward were measured.

Finding: Spatial memory survived up to a year: return visits were ~3x more efficient than naive exploration and direct reward runs resumed ~3x sooner. Although most neurons changed preferred locations, the population pattern at a given location still resembled the one 90 days earlier, and cells whose fields moved tended to stay at the same branching level of the maze. Sessions with better-preserved branching-level tuning were explored more efficiently, and patterns were best preserved near reward. Representations changed less with each repeated experience.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; DOI stem is 10-30 but the bioRxiv API posting date is 11-06. No sample size or inferential statistics are stated in the abstract.
Hippocampal transformations occur along dimensions of memory interference
Soroush Mirjalili et al. (Brice A. Kuhl) · Nature Communications, 2026-11-06 Schema-EpisodicEncoding-Retrieval

Approach: Generated natural-scene images from two categories and characterised visual similarity with neural-network/AI models plus human perceptual and memory judgments, isolating two orthogonal similarity dimensions; then used high-resolution fMRI to measure dimension-specific input-output functions in hippocampus across stages of learning.

Finding: The two visual-similarity dimensions each predicted memory interference, but at different learning stages. Within CA3/dentate gyrus, similarity was inverted (repulsion) exactly on the dimension currently contributing to interference, and preserved on the dimension that was not. Hippocampal representations of similar stimuli are therefore dynamic and re-weighted by whichever similarity axis is behaviourally costly at that moment.

⚠ The dimension-specific repulsion result rests on high-resolution fMRI pattern similarity; the abstract reports no sample size or effect size.
Circuit regulation along an activity continuum shapes flexible and stable representations during learning
D. Yusufi et al. (Y. Gu) · bioRxiv, 2026-11-03 Schema-EpisodicKV-Networks

Approach: Longitudinal two-photon calcium imaging of medial entorhinal cortex in mice learning novel virtual environments, combined with dopaminergic recordings, interneuron manipulation, and behavioural learning measures.

Finding: A persistent axis of excitatory activity predicts representational fate: low-activity neurons activate intermittently, refine strongly with learning, and shift encoding from landmarks toward reward (with dopaminergic pre-reward ramping predicting the shift), while high-activity neurons fire persistently and hold stable spatial maps. Interneurons change in the opposite direction, and transiently activating them on novelty exposure delays excitatory remapping, stabilisation and behavioural learning — i.e. inhibition gates how fast a cognitive map is rewritten.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; the inhibitory-gating claim rests on transient activation during novelty, so a general arousal account is not fully excluded.
KV-Networks
Redundancy maximization as a principle of associative memory learning in Hopfield networks
Mark Blümel et al. (Michael Wibral) · Nature Communications, 2026-11-06 KV-NetworksCross-cutting💻 code available

Approach: Applied Partial Information Decomposition to individual neurons in classical Hopfield networks, decomposing each unit's information into unique, redundant and synergistic contributions from external pattern input versus internal recurrent input; then used redundancy maximisation at each neuron as a local learning objective.

Finding: Below memory capacity, a neuron's activity is dominated by redundancy between the external pattern and the recurrent input, while synergy and unique information stay near zero — until capacity is exceeded and performance collapses. Training each neuron to maximise that redundancy raises capacity from 0.14 to 1.59, a >10-fold gain that also beats recent state-of-the-art Hopfield implementations.

⚠ Capacity gain (0.14 to 1.59) is established in simulation, not in biological data; the redundancy objective is a design principle, not a measured neural quantity.
Oscillatory Phase Coding Emerges in Adaptive RNNs for Flexible Working Memory
K. Lu et al. (S. Wu, H. Luo, F. Fang) · bioRxiv, 2026-11-03 KV-NetworksSchema-Episodic

Approach: Trained adaptive recurrent neural networks on sequence working-memory tasks with variable delays, then ablated task/dynamic components, classified emergent neuron types, analysed recurrent connectivity, and built a reduced circuit model (content units plus an adaptive time ring).

Finding: Oscillatory phase coding emerged without being built in: memorised items recurred at distinct phases of a shared rhythm, with amplitude encoding item identity and phase encoding serial order. Ablations showed three necessary ingredients — neural adaptation, variable retention delay, and flexible readout — and the trained networks spontaneously split into content cells and time cells with phase-organised connections among the latter. The reduced circuit reproduces the effect.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; the phase-code prediction is derived from trained RNNs and a reduced circuit model, without a direct test against recorded neural data.
Encoding-Retrieval
Naturalistic recall preserves temporal specificity despite cortical feature reorganization
S. Peng et al. (B. Levine, B. R. Buchsbaum) · bioRxiv, 2026-11-04 Encoding-RetrievalCross-cuttingSchema-Episodic

Priority item: uses the Sherlock naturalistic viewing-and-free-recall dataset. The cortical gradient reversal is a concrete prediction for encoding-versus-recall alignment analyses.

Approach: Re-analysed fMRI recorded during viewing and free recall of an episode of Sherlock, using data-driven alignment between viewing and recall to identify neural recapitulation of corresponding narrative events; feature levels from low-level visual to semantic were used to predict reinstatement and mapped along the cortical sensory-to-association gradient.

Finding: Recall reinstated corresponding narrative events and carried information distinguishing moments within events, especially in association cortex. All feature levels predicted reinstatement, but differences in predictive performance among feature levels compressed during recall, and their organisation along the sensory-to-association gradient reversed relative to viewing. Within-event temporal specificity survives a substantial reorganisation of feature representations.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; data-driven viewing-recall alignment is correlational, and the abstract reports no sample size or effect size.
The human hippocampus supports auditory coding and multisensory integration
O. Raccah et al. (N. B. Turk-Browne) · PLOS Biology, 2026-11-03 Encoding-Retrieval🖂 data available💻 code available

Naturalistic movie-clip paradigm with high-resolution fMRI; a useful control for RSA/ISC work on audiovisual narratives.

Approach: High-resolution fMRI while participants saw repeated short naturalistic movie clips in four formats — auditory-only, visual-only, congruent audiovisual, incongruent audiovisual — measuring univariate activation and multivariate representations along the longitudinal axis and across hippocampal subfields, plus whole-brain searchlight analyses.

Finding: Univariate analyses detected only visual responses, with no auditory activation and no congruent-stimulus benefit, but multivariate patterns robustly represented both auditory and visual scenes. Posterior hippocampus showed enhanced pattern similarity for congruent stimuli (left-lateralised), while anterior hippocampus supported crossmodal decoding of the same clip across modalities (right-lateralised), implying a more abstract code. Cortical multisensory regions showed parallel effects.

⚠ Univariate null contrasts with multivariate effects depend on ROI definition and decoding choices; no behavioural memory measure is reported in the abstract.
Gaze reinstatement during working memory for natural scenes
Yueying Dong et al. (Anastasia Kiyonaga) · Journal of Neuroscience, 2026-11-06 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Two experiments tracked eye movements while human participants held naturalistic images in working memory, varying whether visual or semantic image properties were task-relevant; time-resolved representational similarity analysis guided by CNN modelling tested the specificity of delay-period gaze patterns.

Finding: Delay-period gaze moved in item-specific spatiotemporal sequences that retraced encoding scanpaths — gaze reinstatement during maintenance, previously described only for long-term retrieval. Gaze representations were more distinct when visual detail was prioritised and ramped up in anticipation of the memory probe, so the oculomotor signal is both content-specific and tuned to when and how the content will be used.

⚠ Gaze reinstatement is correlational with WM content — no causal manipulation of eye movements during maintenance.
Cross-cutting
Amnesia Induced by Suppressing the Focus of Internal Attention
A. Satish et al. (J. C. Hulbert, M. C. Anderson) · bioRxiv, 2026-11-04 Cross-cuttingLLM-Memory

Approach: Behavioural experiments contrasting periods of high versus low external attentional focus and measuring subsequent memory for episodic content activated near in time, with an alternative-reminder test to distinguish passive decay from active suppression.

Finding: High external focus disrupted diverse episodic content activated nearby in time, producing an 'amnesic shadow' for both novel content and already-formed memories. Forgetting exceeded what time alone predicts and generalised to an alternative reminder — the signature of active suppression rather than decay. External attention thus automatically engages a broad inhibition of the systems that generate distracting internal content, including replay-supported episodic retrieval.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed. The behavioural design cannot isolate replay; attributing the amnesic shadow to suppression of an episodic replay system is an inference, not a measurement.
In-group neural alignment serves as a biomarker of social identity
Yohay Zvi et al. (Yaara Yeshurun) · PNAS, 2026-11-05 Cross-cuttingEncoding-Retrieval

Naturalistic video paradigm with an inter-subject correlation measure — ISC here tracks group structure, not only shared stimulus understanding, which is actionable for the ongoing replication analyses.

Approach: fMRI from 42 participants (21 religious, 21 secular) watching neutral and religion-relevant video narratives; inter-subject neural synchrony was computed within groups across content types and brain networks and compared with explicit ratings of emotion, agreement and engagement.

Finding: Explicit measures showed no within-group variance differences, yet the tighter-structured religious group showed significantly higher in-group neural synchrony across all content types and networks, and synchrony predicted religious orientation with up to 92% accuracy. Neural alignment indexes social homogeneity even when behaviour does not.

⚠ n=42 (21 per group); the 92% orientation classification needs an independent held-out replication, and content-specificity claims rest on null explicit-measure differences.
When Model Complexity Matters in Cross-Subject Naturalistic fMRI Prediction
Junhao Zhao et al. (Nan Xu) · arXiv q-bio.NC, 2026-11-02 Cross-cutting

Boundary recovery: posted 2026-11-02, inside the prompt's 7-day lookback but one day before this run's harvest window; missed by the 11-04 run.

Approach: Shared offline cross-subject protocol (fit on training subjects, evaluate held-out subjects without refitting) across HCP movie fMRI (154 subjects), BOLD Moments (10) and Algonauts 2025 (4), comparing raw pixels vs pretrained features, linear vs nonlinear downstream predictors, and stimulus-only, history-only and history-conditioned inputs; plus controlled audiovisual simulations with an explicit hemodynamic model.

Finding: Measured BOLD history gives substantial gains that diminish as rollouts lengthen; nonlinear stimulus-only models can beat linear readouts, but linear autoregression is a strong history-conditioned baseline. Regional cross-subject response consistency predicts held-out accuracy, and less consistent regions gain more from measured history. Simulations show slower hemodynamic responses alone sustain high temporal prediction correlations over longer rollouts — part of the apparent 'memory' is the measurement process.

⚠ arXiv preprint posted 11-02 — one day before this run's window and missed by the 11-04 harvest; recovered here with the date stated. Held-out subjects viewed largely familiar stimuli.
Peripheral
Brain Decoding from a Deep Neural Network of Visual Memory Reveals the Maturation Trajectory of Memory Systems across Ages 3-7
X. Guo et al. (Y. C. Leong, W. A. Bainbridge) · bioRxiv, 2026-11-04 Peripheral

Naturalistic movie-watching fMRI; included as Peripheral because the developmental question is adjacent to, rather than inside, the three active pillars.

Approach: Used a movie-watching fMRI dataset spanning age 3 to adulthood; trained a cross-validated model on adults' movie-evoked activity to predict ResMem (a DNN memorability estimate) for each clip, then applied it to each child's activity while viewing the same movie to derive a 'memory maturation score', followed by structural and searchlight analyses.

Finding: Children showed adult-like neural memory patterns around age 4. Left posterior hippocampal volume — not chronological age or total brain volume — predicted the maturation score, and searchlight analyses traced a sequence: inferior temporal gyrus mature from age 3, left posterior hippocampus by 5, higher-order social-affective and multimodal regions by 7.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; cross-decodability from an adult-trained model is an indirect measure of memory maturation and the structural result is correlational.
