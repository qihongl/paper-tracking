LLM-Memory
Event Memory and Narrative Dynamics: A Large Language Model Framework for Multidimensional State Spaces
Joshua Sariñana (sole author) · arXiv cs.CL, 12 Oct 2026 LLM-Memory Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: Introduces an LLM-based framework for building event representations from autobiographical narratives with excerpt-level traceability — each event-state can be traced back to the text that generated it. Event construction is governed by explicit boundary rules plus a feature schema explicitly derived from hippocampal models of episodic memory and navigation. In a proof-of-concept application, interviews from five participants yielded 256 event-states, embedded via multiple correspondence analysis into a selected 4-D geometry plus 10-D and 13-D reference geometries, with a successor representation modelling expected basin-state occupancy over successive steps.

Finding: Consecutive event-states lay significantly closer in feature space than expected under randomised narrative order — actual narrative transitions follow representational similarity. In the 4-D ten-basin solution, similarity of basin-state feature profiles tracked similarity of one-step routing profiles, with context, mechanism and theme differentiating full-system pathway engagement; centroid-to-successor-profile distance correlations were positive across all five basin solutions. The payoff is methodological: hippocampal principles are converted into source-linked event boundaries, giving an explicit basis for testing neural accounts of event segmentation during naturalistic recall.

⚠ Proof-of-concept on 5 participants; no neural data, so the hippocampal framing is imported rather than tested; multiple-comparison exposure across three geometries.
Role-aware Heuristic Episodic Attention for Conversational LLMs (REA)
Wanyang Hong, Zhaoning Zhang, Yi Chen, Libo Zhang, Baihui Liu, Linbo Qiao, Zhiliang Tian, Dongsheng Li · arXiv cs.CL, 1 Oct 2026 LLM-Memory Cross-cutting

Approach: A context-management framework that assigns different persistence and representation policies to two classes of content. Instructional Memory retains identified global constraints in a dedicated prefix read on every turn; Episodic Memory preserves user inputs verbatim and compresses model replies; heuristic retrieval then selects raw text, a compressed representation, or omission for each historical turn. Three named failure modes are targeted: attention pollution, dilution and drift. Evaluated on Long-MT-Bench+ (60+ turns) across three backbones spanning 1.7B-7B parameters, on bilingual CharacterBench role-playing, and on a distributed state-tracking diagnostic where constraints are spread across turns.

Finding: Judge score improved from 6.32 to 7.36 on a 10-point scale (16.5% relative gain) with mean latency reduced 2.91x. The informative result is the ablation: removing the persistent instruction prefix while keeping episodic retrieval collapsed instruction adherence from 8.18 to 1.95, showing that topical retrieval alone does not preserve global constraints. On distributed state tracking REA reached 0.51 joint goal accuracy where the vanilla baseline fell to 0.05. This is a direct, testable instantiation of the distinction between content retrieval and persistent-state maintenance that Pillar 1 turns on.

⚠ Instruction-recognizer precision only 0.6182 — roughly three in eight prefix commitments are false positives, while a miss drops a genuine constraint; retrieval thresholds tau_low/tau_high were validated on MT-Eval subsets rather than the headline benchmark.
History Replays Itself: Unleashing Long-Context Capability in Linear Attention (NoteBank)
Li Nanbo, Firas Laakom, Yilan Zhang, Piotr Piękos, Yimeng Chen, Liangyu Wang, Yifan Xu, Mateusz Ostaszewski, Dandan Guo, Wenyi Wang, Yanning Dai, Yuhui Wang, Jürgen Schmidhuber · arXiv cs.AI / cs.LG, 11 Oct 2026 LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: Attacks the prevailing assumption that a linear-attention model's fixed-size recurrent state sets its long-context ceiling. NoteBank is a sparse episodic memory that archives selected text before future queries are known, uses the backbone's own features for writing and addressing, and reads retrieved evidence back through the model's native forward computation via replay — a training-free, plug-and-play interface requiring no external embedder, retriever or reader. Evaluated at 1M tokens on six RULER retrieval tasks plus LongBench v1/v2 and MemoryAgentBench, across three 1.3B recurrent backbones, Mamba-2-8B and Qwen3.5-9B.

Finding: At one million tokens, mean RULER accuracy for Gated DeltaNet rose from 1.7% to 70.3% with no significant degradation from 4K to 1M tokens, at ≤0.025% added analytical FLOPs and persistent storage roughly 1/2,600 the size of a matched full-MHA KV cache. The transferable claim is architectural: recurrent state need not carry the whole burden of long-term storage, which is the same division of labour that complementary-learning-systems accounts posit for hippocampus and neocortex.

⚠ Under review at ICLR 2027 — not yet peer reviewed; gains are relative to a weak baseline.

Reduced dorsal CA1 activity limits retention of the temporal component of declarative memory in the Cntnap2 knockout mouse model of autism
Elise Caitlin Rawlinson et al. (Nathalie Dehorter) · eLife, 13 Oct 2026 Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: Used trace fear conditioning in Cntnap2 knockout mice together with fiber photometry and optogenetic manipulation to characterise how dorsal CA1 dysfunction drives specific declarative-memory deficits, then probed generalisation with a separate relational/declarative memory task. The design separates the temporal component (binding stimuli separated by a gap) from the spatial/relational component.

Finding: Temporal binding was reduced in knockouts relative to wildtype, and the photometry/optogenetics link this directly to reduced CA1 activity during the temporal gap — a mechanistic bridge from cellular activity to a memory-retention deficit. On the relational task the knockouts were impaired on flexible spatial memory and preferentially adopted an egocentric strategy, which the authors attribute to an imbalance promoting frontostriatal procedural learning over dCA1-dependent relational memory. Frames dCA1 dysfunction as a circuit-level basis for cognitive inflexibility.

⚠ Single genetic mouse model of a heterogeneous condition; no human or cross-model comparison.
Dopamine synchronizes hippocampal–prefrontal networks
Benedito A de Oliveira-Junior et al. (Rafael N Ruggiero) · Cerebral Cortex, 12 Oct 2026 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: Recorded hippocampus and prefrontal cortex simultaneously in urethane-anaesthetised rats while pharmacologically manipulating dopamine. Compared systemic apomorphine (non-selective agonist) against preferential D1-like (SKF-38393) and D2-like (quinpirole) receptor stimulation, and analysed PFC single units for firing rate, spike-theta phase locking and preferred firing phase.

Finding: Dopamine dose-dependently induced hippocampal-prefrontal theta coupling, including non-zero-lag phase synchrony. In PFC single units it reduced firing rates while strengthening spike-theta phase locking and shifting the preferred firing phase. Neither D1- nor D2-preferential stimulation reproduced the complete dopamine-induced oscillatory pattern, pointing to dopaminergic modulation of HPC-PFC synchrony that is more complex than any single receptor class explains.

⚠ Urethane anaesthesia; no behaving condition and no accompanying memory measure, so the link to consolidation is inferred.
Sleep forms flexible context representations in toddlers
Lisa Bastian, Eva-Maria Kurz, Lilli Gutjahr et al. (Jan Born) · Nature Communications, 12 Oct 2026 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Tests sleep-dependent consolidation at an age when hippocampal function is still immature. Toddlers aged 2-3 were familiarised with two spatial contexts, then had a ~90-minute nap or an equivalent wake interval; memory was probed afterwards with a hide-and-seek relocation game. Sleep physiology was quantified as spindle density and slow-oscillation-spindle phase-amplitude coupling during NREM.

Finding: Only after post-familiarisation sleep did toddlers show significant context memory and stronger toy-context associations than after wakefulness. Contextual memory correlated positively with spindle density and SO-spindle PAC, implying the thalamocortical oscillatory machinery implicated in adult consolidation is already operating — and is behaviourally relevant — well before hippocampal maturation is complete.

⚠ Between-group nap/wake contrast in a small sample; effect sizes not reported in the abstract.
KV-Networks
Neural signatures of serial order access in verbal working memory
Giulia Cristoforetti, Yangwen Xu, Simone Viganò et al. (Wim Fias) · Cerebral Cortex, 12 Oct 2026 KV-Networks Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Tests whether serial position is stored as a spatial code or as something that generalises beyond the encoding layout. Participants memorised digit sequences presented left-to-right or right-to-left — reversing the ordinal-position-to-external-space mapping — while retrieval cues stayed centrally presented during fMRI. Analyses combined direction-specific MVPA decoding, cross-direction decoding, and univariate parametric modulation by serial position.

Finding: Direction-specific decoding appeared in lateral parietal cortex: left inferior parietal cortex carried serial position after left-to-right encoding, right inferior parietal cortex after right-to-left encoding. Cross-direction decoding independently revealed direction-invariant position information in left supramarginal and precentral regions, and univariate activity scaled with serial position in bilateral posterior parietal cortex, left anterior hippocampus, and action-control regions. Serial-order access therefore draws on several coexisting formats at once — a spatially context-dependent code, a layout-invariant ordinal code, and graded process-related activity — which is precisely the representational question Pillar 3 turns on.

Aging and False Memories: Comparing Effects of Item-Relatedness and List Position
Lauri Gurguryan, E A Kensinger, P A Reuter-Lorenz, H R Dimsdale-Zucker · Experimental Aging Research, 10 Oct 2026 KV-Networks Cross-cutting

Approach: A modified Deese-Roediger-McDermott paradigm manipulating semantic and phonological similarity across list position, run in both recall (Experiments 1-2) and item recognition (Experiment 3), with younger and older adults compared directly.

Finding: Older adults recalled fewer items than younger adults, but both groups generated more phonological than semantic false memories, and both produced semantic and phonological false memories from all sublist positions regardless of test format. This challenges the classic mapping of semantic and phonological false memories onto separate long- and short-term stores, and there were no age-related differences in false-memory patterns — the age effect lands on veridical recall, not on the structure of false memory.

Encoding-Retrieval
Electrophysiological correlates of contextual retrieval following virtual reality and desktop-based encoding
Joanna Kisker et al. (Thomas Gruber) · Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 14 Oct 2026 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Tests whether encoding modality is bound as contextual information rather than boosting memory overall. Participants encoded everyday objects in VR and on a desktop, with the encoding task varied across objects, then completed a recognition test with old/new judgments plus source identification for both modality and task. ERPs were decomposed into FN400 (familiarity), LPC (recollection) and LPN (source retrieval).

Finding: Memory performance was comparable across modalities and generally low, with no VR advantage for recognition or source attribution. FN400 old/new effects were similar for both traces, but the LPC old/new effect reached significance only for VR-encoded items — and the direct VR-PC comparison did not. The pattern is compatible with somewhat greater recollection after VR encoding, but the decisive contrast is null; read it as a well-controlled negative result bearing on encoding-specificity claims.

⚠ Low overall accuracy limits headroom for detecting modality effects; direct comparisons appear underpowered.
Neuronal signatures of successful one-shot memory in mid-level visual cortex
Grace F. DiRisio & Cheng Xue (Marlene R. Cohen) · Nature Communications, 12 Oct 2026 Encoding-Retrieval Peripheral

Approach: Two male rhesus macaques performed a continuous image-recognition task of gradually increasing difficulty while V4 populations were recorded, allowing correct and error trials to be compared at matched difficulty. The hypothesis under test is that the perceptual building blocks of one-shot memory reside in mid-level visual cortex, not only in hippocampus and downstream higher areas.

Finding: Four candidate memory signatures were present — magnitude coding, repetition suppression, sparse coding, and population response consistency — but only sparse coding and population response consistency predicted behaviour. Familiar images additionally evoked faster population dynamics, consistent with pattern completion. The result relocates part of the machinery for fast, high-capacity recognition memory into mid-level sensory cortex, operating independently of stimulus features per se, and fits a distributed rather than hippocampus-centric memory architecture.

⚠ n = 2 macaques, both male; no causal manipulation.
Characterizing hippocampal activation during pattern separation with multi-echo fMRI in cognitively normal older adults
Kevin Grant Solar, Sriranga Kashyap, Nicolas Deom et al. (Mary Pat McAndrews) · Brain Imaging and Behavior, 10 Oct 2026 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Hippocampal hyperactivity on the mnemonic similarity task is a candidate AD risk marker, but long scan times have blocked its use in interventional work. The authors ran multi-echo fMRI during the MST — which engages CA3 and dentate gyrus — in 33 cognitively normal older adults and compared activation and behaviour across subsets of eight runs (first four vs second four).

Finding: Behavioural and activation effects were equivalent between the first four and second four runs, indicating robust data can be obtained with four runs in roughly 22 minutes. Left and right CA3+DG activation did not differ. The study delivers an exemplar framework for deriving a single-participant threshold for classifying elevated hippocampal activation — a practical route to individual-level AD-risk stratification in preclinical cohorts. Worth attention given the active AD biomarker programme.

⚠ Small normative sample; the threshold framework is illustrative and needs prospective validation.

Breaking the chains: Toward a neural-level account of episodic memory
Andrej Bicanski (Max-Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences) · Psychological Review 133(5), 1048-1081, Oct 2026 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: A theory paper arguing that episodic memory cannot simply inherit the machinery of spatial memory, because dynamically changing scenes and an immobile agent break the spatial-mapping analogy. The proposed model instead indexes episodes via temporal indices of flexible duration, recruited by a combination of contextual inputs, lateral inhibition, and drive from temporal analogues of grid cells, functioning as an on-demand sequence generator and memory store. Indices learn connections to cortical representations modulated by an amygdalar signal. The architecture is embedded in an extensive review organised along two axes: how it sits relative to other accounts of time coding, serial-order memory and flexible temporal cognition, and how it reconciles with both classical Tulving-style episodic-memory theory and modern RL/generative-model accounts of hippocampal function.

Finding: The model's indices can exhibit both fixed-duration and broadening time fields, the latter resembling classical time cells but unable to index episodes beyond short durations and reminiscent of scalar-expectancy timing codes. Built from biologically plausible, common network motifs, the architecture replays dynamically changing and spatially structured events while the agent is immobile, offers a mechanism for modulating the speed of recall, and can replay disjoint collections of indices — "broken chains" — with temporal order preserved. This is the full theoretical framing for the vector-navigation model published in Current Biology and covered in the 2026-09-23 report.

⚠ Theory and simulation only — no new empirical data; the timing and broken-chain predictions are the testable output.
Peripheral
Do Hopfield networks dream of stored patterns? A statistical-mechanical theory of dreaming in multidirectional associative memories
Adriano Barra, Fabrizio Durante, Andrea Ladiana & Michela Marra Solazzo · Neural Networks, 9 Oct 2026 Peripheral Cross-cutting

Approach: Introduces the Dreaming L-directional Associative Memory (DLAM), a multi-layer Hebbian architecture in which offline "dreaming" and supervised heteroassociative coupling coexist inside a single cost function, placing it in the energy-based-model framework. The replica-symmetric free energy is derived via the Guerra interpolation scheme, yielding self-consistency equations for the order parameters across the control-parameter space; predictions are checked with Monte Carlo simulations for L=3.

Finding: The effective local field decomposes into signal, intra-layer dreaming noise, and inter-layer noise. Dreaming improves retrieval by differentially attenuating high-eigenvalue interference modes of the empirical correlation matrix — suppressing inter-pattern crosstalk while preserving signal. Dreaming and inter-layer coupling are synergistic, opening retrieval regions unreachable by either mechanism alone; the interplay is clearest in pattern disentanglement, where a mixture-state input is split into constituent patterns one per layer, recovering each modality-specific pattern from a cue that blends noisy evidence across sensory channels.

⚠ Pure theory; restricted to the replica-symmetric regime and L=3 simulations.
Standing in unperturbed balance improves spatial memory and facilitates allocentric representation
Achille Pasqualotto, Jun Aoki, Kenji Suzuki & Hideki Kadone · Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, 9 Oct 2026 Peripheral

Approach: Explores the hypothesis that experiencing the world from a sitting posture — as wheelchair users do — yields poorer spatial representation. Forty healthy participants used a novel mobility device permitting transitions between sitting and standing locomotion. Half encoded a large room containing real objects (book, teapot, football, etc.) in the sitting modality and half in the standing modality, then completed a spatial memory task that classified representations as egocentric (viewer-to-object) or allocentric (object-to-object).

Finding: The standing group outperformed the sitting group (p < 0.001) and showed a markedly allocentric spatial representation (p < 0.001), indicating better grasp of the relations among objects. The result suggests posture is not a passive carrier of spatial input but actively scaffolds the reference frame used during encoding.

⚠ Healthy participants standing in for a clinical population — an explicitly stated initial exploration; posture is confounded with the novel device and with locomotion mode.
