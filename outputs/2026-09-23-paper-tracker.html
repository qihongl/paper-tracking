LLM-Memory
Abstention and Noise Filtering: Two Missing Primitives of Softmax Attention
Richard Zhe Wang · arXiv (cs.LG), 18 Sep 2026 LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: Argues that value-pathway gating in attention supplies two distinct capacities that softmax attention by construction lacks: abstention (a head can output nothing, escaping the constraint that weights sum to one) and noise filtering (suppressing interference from superposed features in the residual stream). Matched models from 10M to 350M parameters implement abstention via a learned per-head sink logit in the softmax and filtering via a per-value gate. A controlled-interference probe injects known distractors into the values a head reads and measures what each gate removes.

Finding: The two primitives trade off with scale — abstention accounts for nearly all of the gating benefit at 10M parameters, filtering for most of it at 350M. The best model at every scale has both. Injecting controlled interference confirms the gate removes it, and each gate form has a characteristic blind spot. Both are KV-cache-compatible and add negligible parameters.

⚠ Max scale 350M — the scale crossover is real but far from frontier; no mechanistic account of what the sink logit encodes
Written as a Record, Read as an Address: What a Forward Pass Leaves in an Operation's KV Cache
Lingfeng Wu & Behzad Shomali · arXiv (cs.CL), 21 Sep 2026 LLM-Memory KV-Networks

Approach: Splits a forward pass into a frozen writer (cache recomputed without gradients) and a reader that sees only instruction and operation tokens with all state descriptions hidden, trained in isolation. Anything the reader recovers was therefore already present in the unmodified cache. Tested on a synthetic boxes task ("swap the contents of Box F and Box B") plus ToMi and GSM8K, across Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B, with causal operation-span transplants between worlds holding identical values.

Finding: A base reader recovers ≤0.06 of queried bindings; after isolation training, 0.75–1.00 — and recoverability tracks the operation's read/write footprint. Two access modes emerge: transplants causally redirect which visible state is read (a routing record) even when values are identical, while isolation training additionally opens direct payload access from the single operand-name token in a narrow mid-depth band (layers 12–15 of 32 in Llama-3.1-8B; 14–17 in Mistral-7B). The writing model reads mainly the address, not the value.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; isolation training costs open-book accuracy and is bounded by training coverage
Memory vs. Context? Influential Factors of Factual Recall in Language Models
Guilhem Fouilhé, Nicholas Asher, Philippe Muller · arXiv (cs.CL), 21 Sep 2026 LLM-Memory

Approach: Large-scale replication and stress test of Yu et al. (2023) on how LMs arbitrate between memorized knowledge and contradictory in-context statements. World-capitals experiments rerun on 31 models spanning Pythia, GPT-2, Qwen3 and Ministral families (base and post-trained), extended to five additional knowledge relation types from ParaConflict.

Finding: Most original findings hold — larger models and higher-frequency entities favour memorized answers, with substantial family-level variance. But generalization is poor in specific, informative ways: entity-frequency effects vanish on Qwen3-14B and 32B; post-training shifts the memory–context trade-off inconsistently across families; and question phrasing alone moves reliance on memorized knowledge by up to 80 percentage points. Semantically unrelated prose can mimic coherent supporting context.

⚠ Purely behavioural — no attention or KV-level analysis; the 80-point phrasing effect is reported without a mechanistic account

Prefrontal control of hippocampal map reorganization during sleep
Ruoyu Huang, Masahiro Nakano, Claudia Clopath, Jan Born, Thorsten Bartsch, Martin Both, Eduardo Blanco-Hernandez, Andrea Burgalossi · bioRxiv (posted 21 Sep 2026), Univ. Tübingen Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Miniscope Ca²⁺ imaging of mouse CA1 place cells as animals explored a familiar environment followed by a similar novel one. Post-encoding sleep versus wake was contrasted, and mPFC was chemogenetically inactivated during post-encoding sleep. Read-outs were place-map reinstatement in the novel context plus behavioural familiarity indices (exploration, rearing).

Finding: Place cells remapped in the novel context as expected — but a single episode of post-encoding sleep reversed the remapping, reinstating familiar place-cell representations in the novel environment, an effect absent in the wake condition. Neural reinstatement was accompanied by reduced exploration and rearing. mPFC inactivation during post-encoding sleep abolished the generalization and instead preserved the novel map. Sleep therefore does not merely stabilize wake-formed hippocampal patterns; it actively reshapes them under prefrontal control.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; N not stated in the abstract and the causal claim rests on a single chemogenetic manipulation
Transient excitability and synaptic consolidation support stable memory despite neural drift
Surbhit Wagle, Claudia Clopath · bioRxiv (posted 21 Sep 2026), Imperial College London Schema-Episodic KV-Networks

Approach: Two-region recurrent network model of hippocampus (HPC) and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) with region-specific synaptic plasticity, excitability-dependent neuronal allocation, HPC→cortical coupling and activity-dependent intrinsic plasticity. Regions were run independently and as an intact circuit; simulated erasure of learning-induced potentiation tested the time course of hippocampal dependence.

Finding: In isolation, faster HPC synaptic turnover produced pronounced drift while persistent ACC connectivity stayed stable. In the intact circuit, hippocampal input recruited ACC neurons but also propagated hippocampal variability into cortex, destabilizing the emerging cortical engram and impairing memory expression — drift is contagious, not merely local. A transient increase in intrinsic excitability of recruited ACC neurons rescued this by promoting repeated reactivation during an early consolidation window, stabilizing ensemble membership without preventing hippocampal drift. Simulated erasure reproduced early hippocampal dependence that ACC plasticity did not share.

⚠ Purely computational — no new neural or behavioural data; the excitability window is a model assumption fitted to prior literature
A vector navigation and inference architecture can construct universal cognitive maps for abstract reasoning
Andrej Bicanski · Current Biology, 22 Sep 2026 Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: A model showing how existing spatial-navigation architectures (vector navigation plus non-local inference) can iteratively construct cognitive maps over arbitrary stimulus dimensions, using the same grid-cell manifolds that support spatial representation. The model is probed for noise tolerance, handling of discontinuous stimulus spaces, and transfer to abstract reasoning tasks; neural-level implementations are proposed for several components.

Finding: The same computational modules that build a map also support analogy-making, subspace construction and perspective-taking — abstract reasoning falls out of the mapping machinery rather than requiring separate mechanisms. Noise tolerance matches behavioural data, discontinuous stimulus spaces are accommodated, and the 1D attributes to be combined can be isolated upstream of entorhinal cortex.

⚠ Single-author theory paper; neural implementations are proposed, not tested against recordings
Gradual evolution of multiplexed spatial coding within a conserved hippocampal scaffold
Xiao-Fan Ge, Chu Deng, … Li Lu · bioRxiv, 19 Sep 2026, Kunming Institute of Zoology / CUHK Schema-Episodic Cross-cutting

Approach: CA1 recordings from rats and tree shrews (Tupaia belangeri chinensis) — a mammal phylogenetically and ecologically intermediate between rodents and primates — using identical tasks and analyses. Position selectivity, non-positional tuning and multiplexing were quantified, and decoding accuracy compared against cell-count matched populations.

Finding: Rat CA1 was position-dominant; tree shrew CA1 showed a hybrid code — weaker position selectivity, stronger non-positional tuning, and prevalent multiplexing approaching primate characteristics. Multiplexed populations decoded location as accurately as rat position populations while using fewer neurons, implying a computational advantage to high-dimensional coding. Despite this representational shift, the proximo-distal gradient, pattern completion and global remapping all persisted: representational format can be tuned without altering the underlying circuit.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; N not stated in the abstract; "fewer neurons" advantage is demonstrated only for location decoding
KV-Networks
Latest Exact Match Attention
Moritz Brösamle · arXiv (cs.LG), 22 Sep 2026 KV-Networks LLM-Memory

Approach: Introduces LEMA: queries and keys are binarized and each query attends only to the latest exactly matching key. Proves LEMA transformers with chain-of-thought can simulate word-RAMs, and — the direction prior hard-attention variants lacked — that word-RAMs can simulate LEMA at per-token cost independent of context length. Training uses a straight-through estimator for binarization plus a soft-attention surrogate annealed toward LEMA. Evaluated on synthetic associative recall, repeated rare phrases and needle retrieval, with LMs scaled to 834M parameters.

Finding: LEMA models use their growing state to store and recall a large number of associations, outperforming gated DeltaNet with its fixed state size. At 834M they match softmax transformers of roughly half the size in loss; on repeated rare phrases and needle retrieval they remain behind softmax transformers but recall across longer distances than comparable-size GDN. Dictionary-based inference gives constant generation speed with dictionaries in main memory rather than VRAM.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; 834M is a small scaling test, and LEMA still trails softmax attention on the retrieval tasks that matter most
ARM: Attention with Routed-Memory for Learnable Sparse Control
Qiuhao Zeng, Jerry Huang, Peng Lu, … Charles Ling, Gang Niu, Boyu Wang · arXiv (cs.LG), 21 Sep 2026 · ICML 2026 KV-Networks LLM-Memory

Approach: Replaces hard KV eviction/pruning with a fully differentiable fixed-size memory organized as a hierarchical router. A Gumbel-Softmax selects memory slots and sigmoid-gated updates softly combine new and stored information, so nothing is discarded outright. A trained policy dynamically varies how much memory is accessed at inference.

Finding: Soft gated updates avoid the information loss that hard eviction incurs, and the adaptive access policy lets the model spend more memory on inputs requiring deeper reasoning. Reports superior performance and efficiency versus fixed KV-caching approaches on commonsense and long-context reasoning benchmarks, with favourable memory and latency scaling.

⚠ Retained here because it makes an explicit retention-vs-eviction claim, but evaluation is benchmark accuracy only — no analysis of what is actually retained or forgotten
Sequence learning as Bayesian filtering
Dennis Norris, Kristjan Kalm · Psychological Review, 21 Sep 2026 KV-Networks Cross-cutting

Approach: A model of sequence learning and recall in which memory's function is to maintain an up-to-date representation of the environment as a prior, combined with short-term-memory information to form a posterior that drives recall and then updates the priors — a Bayesian filter. Applied to Hebb's (1961) task, in which participants learn sequences over repeated presentations embedded in immediate serial recall.

Finding: The single prediction-update cycle simulates a wide swathe of Hebb-effect data: learning multiple lists simultaneously, list-spacing effects, the differential impact of variation at list beginning versus end, learning from response errors, interference between similar lists, and repetition effects on forward versus backward recall. The phenomena follow directly from the basic computational principles rather than from added mechanisms.

⚠ Behavioural fits only — no neural data; parsimony is the argument, and alternative filter formulations are not contrasted
Encoding-Retrieval
Drift-diffusion dynamics of hippocampal replay
Zhongxuan Wu, Xue-Xin Wei · PLOS Computational Biology, 22 Sep 2026 Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: A computational framework modelling replay during sharp-wave ripples as a drift-diffusion process, allowing switching between several well-motivated dynamic types to capture population-level heterogeneity. Applied to rat hippocampal recordings; diagnostics include stationarity fraction, trajectory speed versus the animal's movement speed, and mean-squared displacement scaling.

Finding: Three results matter. A small fraction of SWRs are stationary, and those with drift-diffusion dynamics move much faster than the animal did. Replay dynamics are heterogeneous and inconsistent with the previously proposed random-walk model — mean-squared displacement scales quadratically with time, indicating substantial drift. And fewer than 1% of SWR events show sequential structure at the 100 ms timescale before the animal had spatial experience in the environment, arguing that pre-existing sequential structure is not simply awaiting expression.

⚠ Method is validated on existing datasets without ground truth; the quadratic-MSD claim depends on the model's switching assumptions
CA1 reinstatement and novelty response independently support recognition memory
Joern Alexander Quent, Mengya Zhang, Deniz Vatansever · bioRxiv (posted 21 Sep 2026), Fudan University Encoding-Retrieval Schema-Episodic

Approach: Re-analyses the Natural Scenes Dataset — 8 participants, a continuous recognition memory task spanning 41 separate days, 213,000 trials total. Reinstatement and novelty indices were computed per image and related to successful memory encoding, with each index tested while controlling for the other. Exploratory analyses extended to CA3.

Finding: CA1 showed encoding-related reinstatement and novelty processing that survived mutual control — consistency and differentiation are orthogonal, independently contributing signals coexisting within the same subfield. Reinstatement was specific to retrieving the initial representation on the first retrieval attempt and did not generalize to other reinstatement forms. CA3 showed reinstatement with a different response profile.

⚠ Preprint, not peer reviewed; n = 8 participants, and the CA3 extension is explicitly exploratory
The Quasi-Systematic Nature of Splitter Cells
Naomi Chaix-Eichel, Snigdha Dagar, Frédéric Alexandre, Nicolas P. Rougier · Neural Computation, 22 Sep 2026 Encoding-Retrieval KV-Networks

Approach: Tests whether a random recurrent structure is sufficient to generate splitter cells — cells firing differentially at the same location depending on past or future trajectory. An agent with egocentric sensory inputs navigates and alternates choices at intersections; identified splitter cells are then systematically lesioned and behaviour re-measured, with position, orientation and decision decoded from reservoir activity.

Finding: Splitter cells emerge spontaneously from random recurrence, consistent with temporal-sequence accounts of spatial firing. Critically, lesioning them left behaviour intact: in most cases new splitter cells re-emerged through network reorganization, and otherwise the task was solved with no detectable splitter cells at all. Position, orientation and decision remained decodable after repeated lesioning.

⚠ Model only — no neural data; the lesion result shows sufficiency is not necessity, which is a claim about the model rather than about hippocampus
Cross-cutting
Misinformation as an Event-Model Problem
Markus Huff, Jan Pascal Göbel, Helen Fischer · Perspectives on Psychological Science, 21 Sep 2026 Cross-cutting Schema-Episodic

Approach: Conceptual argument, not new data. Reframes the continued-influence effect: because misinformation is typically encountered inside temporally unfolding narratives, it is integrated into event-structured mental models where it acquires a causal role supporting prediction, explanation and inference — not merely a proposition accepted or rejected.

Finding: The framework's key prediction is that corrections which merely negate false information remove a causal element without restoring model completeness, leaving incoherent representations that invite compensatory inference and allow the misinformation to re-emerge. Corrections that align with event boundaries and supply factually accurate, causally adequate replacements should stabilize updated representations. It unifies continued influence, delayed resurgence, and inference-based false memory under one mechanism.

⚠ Theory piece with no new experiments — the central prediction (causally adequate replacements aligned to event boundaries) is untested here
Peripheral
Uncovering latent cognitive, metacognitive, and neural bases of problem-solving deficits in children with developmental dyscalculia
O.H.M. Lasnick, Yunji Park, Percy Mistry, … Vinod Menon · Journal of Neuroscience, 21 Sep 2026, Stanford Peripheral Cross-cutting

Approach: Integrates Bayesian computational modelling with whole-brain decoding in 68 children aged 8–10 (38 female), comparing developmental dyscalculia (DD) with typically achieving peers. Latent parameters quantify strategy execution efficiency (counting, retrieval, decomposition) and metacognitive control (switching time between strategies, sensitivity to problem difficulty, adaptivity of strategy selection), linked to neural measures via ElasticNet decoding.

Finding: Children with DD were impaired across all strategies, not one — reduced execution efficiency plus longer baseline switching times, blunted difficulty sensitivity and lower adaptivity. Whole-brain decoding distinguished DD from peers on distributed frontoparietal, hippocampal and ventral-visual patterns where univariate analysis did not, and predicted individual working efficiency. The authors read this as multidimensional neural disruption rather than a simple numerical deficit.

⚠ Cross-sectional with no intervention — the decoding result is correlational and the hippocampal contribution is not isolated causally
