LLM-Memory
The Sleeping Agent: What Gist-Based Context Compression Loses and Why
Kyrkewood NE · arXiv, 2026-08-12 LLM-Memory Cross-cutting

Approach: Uses Salience-Weighted Consolidation (SWC), a biologically-inspired compression framework motivated by sleep-based memory consolidation, as a diagnostic probe to study when gist-based context compression helps versus hurts long-horizon LLM-agent memory. Evaluated across all 10 LoCoMo conversations (1,935 matched text-only questions, 1,501 in the primary aggregate).

Finding: Gist compression substantially outperforms truncation on multi-hop reasoning and single-hop factual questions, but temporal questions degrade sharply under compression. The failure is traced mechanistically to the gist-abstraction prompt preserving relational/event structure while discarding dates and times. A one-sentence prompt modification raised temporal-expression preservation ~20× (3.05% → 62.39%) with negligible change to entity/event preservation, recovering +0.314 [0.254, 0.375] judge accuracy on temporal questions — a precision instrument for the lingering-memory vs consolidation trade-off.

Learning to Recall with Transformers Beyond Orthogonal Embeddings
Vural NM, Bietti A, Soltanolkotabi M, Wu D · ICLR 2026 LLM-Memory

Approach: Theory paper analyzing a single-layer transformer with random (non-orthogonal) token embeddings trained by empirical gradient descent on a token-retrieval task, where the model must identify an informative token in a length-L sequence and learn a one-to-one token→label mapping. Tracks the early phase of GD and complements analysis with a lower bound for the underlying statistical problem.

Finding: Yields explicit formulas for storage capacity revealing a multiplicative dependence among sample size N, embedding dimension d, and sequence length L — quantitatively different from the idealized orthogonal-embedding regime. Numerical validation confirms the scaling, and the lower bound shows this multiplicative rate is intrinsic under non-orthogonal embeddings. Directly informs factual-recall capacity limits of real LLMs.

Schema-Episodic
Developmentally defined subcircuits in the hippocampus support spatial learning
Huszár R, … Buzsáki G · Neuron, 2026 Aug 12 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Combined embryonic birthdating of interconnected CA3–CA1 neurons with electrophysiology in a place-reward association task in mice, examining how parallel developmentally defined subcircuits along the trisynaptic pathway support spatial memory.

Finding: Learning reorganized correlation patterns near rewarded locations; these patterns were reactivated during sleep and predicted subsequent memory retrieval. Neurons born on the same embryonic day exhibited correlated activity across brain states and subfields. Implicates developmentally defined parallel hippocampal subcircuits — and offline reactivation — in the encoding and retrieval of spatial/episodic memory.

Cross-region neuron co-firing mediated by ripple oscillations supports distributed working memory representations
Verzhbinsky IA, … Rutishauser U, Halgren E · Nature Neuroscience, 2026 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Human intracranial microwire recordings across hippocampus, amygdala, vmPFC, ACC, and pre-SMA bilaterally during a working-memory task, analyzing ripple-oscillation co-occurrence and cross-region neuronal co-firing.

Finding: Ripple rates increased in all recorded regions during encoding, maintenance, and retrieval. Co-occurring ripples increased cross-region co-firing by ~30% without decrement over distances up to 220 mm, and scaled with memory load during maintenance and retrieval. During retrieval, co-ripples promoted reinstatement of stimulus-specific, long-distance co-firing patterns observed at encoding — especially during rapid recognition. Shows ripple oscillations coordinate long-range, stimulus-specific co-firing supporting distributed WM representations.

Extinction-restraint intervention eradicates fear memory encoded in BLA engram cells via D2 receptor-mediated dopaminergic modulation from locus coeruleus
Li T, … Zhang P, Tian B · Experimental & Molecular Medicine, 2026 Schema-Episodic Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: PTSD-related fear-relapse mouse model testing an extinction-restraint (Ext-Res) intervention; combined chemogenetic/circuit manipulation of basolateral amygdala (BLA) engram cells and locus coeruleus (LC) tyrosine-hydroxylase-positive neurons.

Finding: Reactivation of BLA engram cells correlated with relapse after fear reinstatement, and post-extinction BLA-engram activation induced relapse. Ext-Res protected against relapse and facilitated BLA-engram reactivation. Dopamine (not norepinephrine) released from LC→BLA, acting via D2 receptors, deactivated the engram; LC–BLA circuit manipulation replicated or reversed Ext-Res effects. Reveals a D2-dependent dopaminergic circuit mechanism for erasing fear-memory relapse.

Encoding-Retrieval
Implementing Manipulation in the Face of Distraction: Spatiotemporal and Spectral Dynamics in Visual Working Memory
Luo C, … Li J · NeuroImage, 2026 Aug 12 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Recorded EEG in healthy adults performing a visual working-memory task in which "manipulation" (transforming goal-relevant info) and "distraction" (irrelevant input) were orthogonally crossed; used individualized representational analyses.

Finding: Manipulation and distraction each increased response times and decreased accuracy, with no significant interaction. Neurally, the manipulation-related representation remained intact regardless of distraction, whereas distraction specifically disrupted maintenance-related activity. Dissociates the neural demands of implementing manipulation from resisting distraction in WM.

Temporal effects of exercise on retrieval-induced forgetting
Loprinzi PD, … Storm BC · Psychological Research, 2026 Aug 11 Encoding-Retrieval

Approach: Direct replication and extension (N=372 healthy young adults) of retrieval-induced forgetting (RIF) using a standard category-exemplar task with selective retrieval practice and two final test assessments; acute aerobic exercise was performed between the two tests.

Finding: Replicated the RIF effect (selective retrieval impairs later recall of related non-retrieved items). Critically, exercise performed between tests preserved the RIF effect over time relative to a no-exercise comparison, supporting a protective effect of acute exercise on the inhibitory/retrieval processes underlying RIF.

Cross-cutting
Neural reactivation supports memory formation when events end
Silva M, … Fuentemilla L · Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2026 Aug 12 Cross-cutting

Approach: Review synthesizing a growing body of evidence across tasks and recording modalities on rapid neural reactivation occurring at the termination of an event, describing when it occurs, how it is measured, and its neural signatures.

Finding: End-of-event reactivation — rapid reinstatement of just-encoded representations — is a robust and functionally meaningful phenomenon. The authors propose it as an efficient neural mechanism that structures continuous experience into discrete, retrievable episodes, bridging event segmentation and memory formation. Useful theoretical scaffolding for the episodic-memory and consolidation pillars.

Peripheral
Protective role of hemokinin-1 against age-dependent spatial memory and attention decline in mice
Tajti BT, … Borbély É · GeroScience, 2026 Aug 12 Peripheral

Approach: Longitudinal and cross-sectional comparison of young (3–5 mo), middle-aged (12–15 mo), and old (18–20 mo) wild-type and hemokinin-1 (HK-1 / Tac4)-deficient C57BL/6J mice on complementary cognitive tasks.

Finding: Endogenous HK-1 modulates age-dependent cognitive impairment: deficient mice showed impaired spatial memory and attention with age, implicating Tac4/neuroimmune signaling in protecting against aging-related memory decline. Relevant to the lab's aging and spatial-memory interests, though at the molecular/rodent level rather than computational mechanism.
